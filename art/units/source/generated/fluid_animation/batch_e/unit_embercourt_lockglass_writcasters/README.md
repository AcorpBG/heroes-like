# Lockglass Writcasters action sources

This directory owns only `unit_embercourt_lockglass_writcasters`. The source identity is the original red-and-ivory two-armed rifle bearer, with two human legs and one bronze/darkwood lockglass rifle. The source packing and original idle paintings remain authoritative. The actual gameplay role is ranged; support is a physical acknowledgement rather than a new magic ability.

`prepare.py` reconstructs action-specific guides at a fixed anatomical scale from original source pixels. Each action has its own prompt, seed and H3 recipe. `run_generation.py` separates retained latent sampling from tiled VAE decode; `pipeline.py` serializes bounded complete stages under the shared GPU lock. No provider/model/slot changes are made. `segment.py` verifies pinned local BiRefNet model and code hashes before extracting semantic alpha, preserving original RGB for opaque pixels.

`correct_move.py` adds the retained original near-leg forward master to the corrected reciprocal gait take; the rejected far-leg-only video remains intact. `correct_cast.py` supplies the retained original chest-salute master before support submission. Each guide has its complete imagegen prompt, reference hashes and fixed anatomical registration. Run those preparation corrections only for their unsubmitted takes. `pipeline.py --one` processes the corrected movement alone and exits at a bounded checkpoint.

`correct_death.py` removes the always-gripped-rifle contradiction from the original release/fall guides and grounds the lying body's near-forearm contact. The dropped rifle/tag has a foreground footprint below that point; using its lowest dangling pixel as body ground made the original guide hover. The whole original reference is registered once before sampling, with the same canvas, extraction scale and video origin.

`correct_attack.py` retains the rejected first stock-shove take and adds the original `attack_return_v1.png` master: both visible gloves hold the same rifle in front of the chest. The failed take raises the gun behind the head while the hands settle early; a full guided second action is required instead of hiding the recovery with a frame jump. Complete failed videos, latents, prompts and recipes remain retained.

`pipeline_grouped.py` processes no more than two complete original action sources per GPU lease, awaiting all sampling and all decode terminal completion before releasing. A separate bounded lease mattes at most those two actions, with CPU review pauses outside both locks. `refine_edges.py` preserves the initial semantic recipe and recovers reviewed plate mixtures at its two-pixel contour without changing opaque RGB or source registration.

`correct_move_plate.py` preserves the second reciprocal-gait take after actual native review exposed an opaque magenta rim. Its original video plate is green/yellow in the affected frames, while the transparent guide subject has no opaque magenta. The original far-step guide also has visible green hair/weapon spill. The retained image-tool repair `far_step_clean_v1.png` preserves that pose, two grips, original dimensions and ground registration with clean contours; its full prompt and hashes are retained. The third movement take uses unchanged ready/near-step RGBA and this clean far-step guide, retaining anatomical registration, guide timing, seed and sampling parameters on a neutral gray composite plate. This corrects H3 conditioning rather than removing or recoloring opaque generated artwork. `movement_plate_correction.json` records the source hashes and measured guide-edge evidence.

`segment_uniform.py` retains the exact earlier uniform-plate extraction tool. The corrected attack's frame11 has a reviewed spatial blue/magenta gradient. `segment.py` requires confidently excluded corners and samples the nearest excluded background at least three pixels from the semantic contour for such spatial plates; opaque RGB stays unchanged. Uniform plates retain their existing spread assertion and contour rule.

Each take preserves original FFV1 RGB, decoded frame hashes, MP4, latent, guides, prompt, workflow and sampling/decode history. Matting is rebuildable. Failed sources remain retained and must never be silently overwritten. Pending output is not publication acceptance.

After personal original chronological/enlarged and native/mirrored/action/map review, `select_reviewed.py` records exact observed source indices and timing. `publish_unit.py` holds the shared content lock and proves all other current unit rows remain unchanged. `verify_delivery.py` compares every packed pose against original pixels and verifies unchanged baseline idle/map pixels and timing. `commit_unit.py` stages only this unit and its UID replacement against current HEAD, preserving other uncommitted rows.

No Linux or whole-game validation is implied by these Windows focused fixtures. Native phase captures and chronological review are distinct from continuous source playback or a manual game session.

The accepted delivery has move34, attack34, ranged30, hit13, defend19, cast24 and death34 original H3 poses: 188 new poses, plus the unchanged eight-pose idle. The dead alias uses the final death pose. `completion.json` records source/import/runtime acceptance and byte-verified cleanup. `finalize_review.py` rebuilds reviewed selection metadata and verifies that publication leaves the accepted artwork byte-identical. All ten complete original takes remain retained, including the rejected first movement, second movement and first stock-strike sources.

The final live normal and Shoot fixtures each passed 678 checks. The first live strike observer missed contact pose18; its unchanged warm retry observed all34 poses. The source, authored timing and strict assertions were not changed to obtain the pass. Temporary visual reviews and test exports are removed by `cleanup_owned.py`; source recipes and tools reconstruct them when needed.
