Image 1 is a painting of an industrial town on both banks of a glowing slag river in a basalt canyon at dusk. Turn it into the same canyon before the town was built: wild, untouched ground with only the slag river running through it.

Remove every building and built structure: the clock hall with its bell, the gatehouse and the dam with its walls, the crucible tower, the chapel with its bell tower, the counting house with the round vault door, the warehouse, the market stalls and the scales on their frame, the shield foundry, the artillery bay and its mortars, the kennels, the long shed with the crawler engine, the giant furnace idol and its shrine, the rail viaduct, the bridges, the crane, the big building and pipes at the lower right, the stairs and walls. The slag waterfall that poured through the dam now runs down the rocks of the canyon.

Where each of them stood, paint the natural ground that would be there: uneven basalt shelves and spurs, scree, boulders, rough spoil heaps and dark ash-grey earth, with a few dead shrubs. Nothing may look like a prepared plot: no paving, no foundations, no outlines, no markers, no stakes, no stacked materials.

Keep exactly as they are: the camera and its perspective, the shape of the canyon and its walls, the winding slag river and its glow, the old mine lift on the cliff at the upper left, the smoky dusk sky, the sunset at the far end of the canyon, the colours and the painted brushwork. The foreground rocks at the bottom stay as they are.

The stone is dark basalt dusted with ash grey; there is no snow or frost anywhere. No text, no lettering, no people.
