Use: the town screen background of an original fantasy strategy game, in the spirit of classic hand-painted 1990s strategy-game town screens but entirely original. One coherent painting with real depth and volume.

Scene: Brasshollow, a mining town that rules by contract: heavy, angular, hot and audited. It sits on both banks of a river of glowing slag that winds through a basalt canyon at dusk. Brass ore lifts climb the canyon walls, gantry cranes reach over loading yards, iron bridges and a rail viaduct cross the slag river, furnace pits glow on the banks, warning banners in charcoal and brass hang from iron braces, and pale powdery chalk dust lies on the stone.

Camera and depth: an elevated oblique view from high on one canyon wall, about 30 degrees down, with strong perspective. The slag river runs diagonally from the lower left to the upper right and disappears into the haze, so the canyon recedes deep into the picture. Buildings on the near bank at the frame edges are large and partly cut off; buildings on the far bank and further up the canyon get smaller, hazier and bluer. Buildings are turned at different angles, showing two sides, following the bends of the river. There are no regular terraces, no rows and no grid: the town looks grown, not planned, on uneven rock shelves, spurs and spoil heaps at different heights.

Landmarks: in the middle distance, on a rock spur on the far bank, the main contract hall with a clock face and a great brass bell, the clear focal point. Further up the canyon, a fortified gatehouse whose walls cross the river on a dam. Around them, clustered irregularly at different distances: a mage-engineers' tower with a glowing crucible, a boiler chapel with a bell tower, a counting house with a round vault door, a shield foundry, an artillery bay with mortar barrels, kennels for mechanical dogs, a long shed holding a tracked crawler engine, a rail office on the viaduct, a warehouse, a market yard with great scales, and a furnace shrine where a giant walking furnace idol stands. Each building stays readable as its own building with a clear silhouette; where one stands in front of another it may overlap it a little.

Frame: the top 12% is smoky dusk sky and far canyon walls. The bottom 18% is dark foreground rock and slag, and the lower-left corner holds no buildings.

Light: a warm, low sunset at the far end of the canyon; deep blue-grey shadows on the walls turned toward the viewer, rim light along roof edges, hot orange light from windows, furnaces and the slag river. Smoke and heat haze build up with distance.

Style: rich hand-painted digital matte painting with visible brush texture and shapes that read at small size. Industrial but grounded: riveted iron plates, braces, pipes, gauges, few decorative gears. No outlines, no cel shading, no isometric game-icon look, not photoreal. Palette: charcoal, iron blue, brass, heated orange, ash grey, chalk white; brass is an accent, not the whole picture.

No text, no lettering, no interface elements, no people in the foreground.
