Use: the town screen background of an original fantasy strategy game, in the spirit of classic hand-painted 1990s strategy-game town screens but entirely original. One coherent painting with real depth and volume.

Scene: Bellwake Harbor, the home port of the Veilmourn Armada, fog-bound funeral fleets that steal sea routes and keep the memories of the drowned: solemn, salt-worn, quiet and secretive. The town grows on both shores of a narrow sea fjord between black sea cliffs at blue dusk. Black-sailed funeral ships with pale lanterns ride at their moorings along timber piers and old stone quays, bronze bells hang from belfries, gantries and mooring posts, round mirrors of polished silver glass catch the last light, and low fog lies on the water.

Camera and depth: an elevated oblique view from high on one cliff, about 30 degrees down, with strong perspective. The fjord runs diagonally from the lower left to the upper right and opens onto the sea in the far haze, so the town recedes deep into the picture. Buildings on the near shore at the frame edges are large and partly cut off; buildings on the far shore and further up the fjord get smaller, hazier and bluer. Buildings are turned at different angles, showing two sides, following the bends of the shore. There are no regular terraces, no rows and no grid: the town looks grown, not planned, on uneven rock ledges, spurs and shingle at different heights, with stairs cut into the rock.

Landmarks: in the middle distance, on a tall sea stack in the fjord joined to the far shore by an arched stone bridge, the bell hall: a tall dark hall with a great open belfry of many bronze bells, the clear focal point. At the mouth of the fjord, a sea wall with two squat towers and a great chain boom across the water. Around them, clustered irregularly at different distances: a lighthouse tower crowned with a ring of mirrors, a covered slipway shed where a ship with a silvered mirror hull is being built, a funeral chapel hung with lanterns, a vault of salt-white stone with a heavy round door, a chart house under a small dome, a harpoon station with giant harpoons on racks, a long rope shed with hooks and nets, a quay market with awnings and salvage, a salvage warehouse, a tavern, and a flooded sea pen where a vast pale leviathan rests half-submerged. Each building stays readable as its own building with a clear silhouette; where one stands in front of another it may overlap it a little.

Frame: the top 12% is foggy dusk sky and far sea cliffs. The bottom 18% is dark foreground rock, shingle and water, and the lower-left corner holds no buildings. Beyond the mouth of the fjord there is only open sea, sea stacks and fog, no other town.

Light: a cold, low, pale-gold glow on the sea horizon at the far end of the fjord, under a slate-blue and violet dusk sky; deep blue-grey shadows on the cliffs and walls turned toward the viewer, rim light along roof edges, warm amber light from windows and lanterns, and a few cold white glints from the mirrors. The fog thickens with distance and lies low on the water, but every building stays readable and the picture is not murky.

Style: rich hand-painted digital matte painting with visible brush texture and shapes that read at small size. Weathered maritime gothic: tarred timber, black slate roofs, salt-white stone, verdigris bronze, rope and chains, and the bone-white ribs of old ships reused as arches; restrained, not ornate. No outlines, no cel shading, no isometric game-icon look, not photoreal. Palette: slate blue, fog grey, tar black, salt white, verdigris bronze and lantern amber; amber is an accent, not the whole picture.
