Inside the masked area only, paint the spore choir hall of the Rotchoir Cantors: a tall hall of weathered pale grey timber, its roof almost covered by great bone-pale bracket fungi and glowing green spore caps, with a ring of hanging gongs in its open front. It is turned about thirty degrees to the right, so its front and left side show. It stands on the mossy hummock in the middle distance, on stilts of dark timber driven into the mud and reeds, with a short crooked plank walk leading to it. Its timber and thatch are clearly lighter than the dark swamp around it and catch the dusk light, so its whole outline stands out. It fills the masked area, but no part of it touches the edges of the masked area.

Materials and mood: the town of predatory bog clans who rule by ferry chains, drum law and the hunt: dark weathered timber bound with rope and rusted chain, steep shaggy roofs of thatch and black shingles, bone-white horns, antlers and tusks, hides, moss and reeds, green glass witch-lanterns, orange fire light in doorways. Savage, watchful and weathered, restrained, not cartoonish; not a castle, not a horror film.

Paint it matched to image 1: the same perspective and scale for that distance and loose painterly brushwork. The pale gold dusk glow is low and far away at the upper right, so fronts facing the viewer are in soft olive shade, still clearly lighter than the swamp behind them, with a gold rim on roof edges, horns and right-hand sides, lit from within by its own fires and lanterns, and a soft shadow falls toward the viewer and to the left. It is in the middle distance, in a thin green mist. It must look painted into the scene, readable against the dark swamp.

Keep the reeds, moss, mud, water, trees and mist around it as crisp and detailed as the rest of image 1; do not soften or blur them. No snow or frost. No smoke plumes. No text, lettering or numbers. No people and no animals.
