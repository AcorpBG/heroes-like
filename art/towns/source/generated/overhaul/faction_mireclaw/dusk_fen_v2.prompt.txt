Image 1 is a painting of a vast wild swamp at a sickly gold-green dusk, seen from high in the crown of a colossal cypress on the right: still black pools, mossy hummocks and root mounds, giant moss-hung cypresses fading into mist, and the pale gold glow low at the upper right. Repaint it as the same swamp from the same viewpoint, with one change: its ground is drawn with thousands of thin upright reed strokes and their thin upright reflections, a fine vertical hatching all over the picture. Take that hatching away.

Paint the swamp floor in broad, soft, varied masses instead: rounded cushions of moss, low mounds of matted grass painted as soft clumps rather than separate blades, smooth mud flats, tangled roots and rotting fallen logs, patches of green duckweed and round lily pads lying flat on the water, and calm dark water with soft, blurred, horizontal reflections and a few gold glints. Keep reeds and cattails only as a few distinct tufts and small stands here and there, painted as soft masses with a few leaning blades, never as rows of parallel upright lines. Large shapes of light and shadow carry the ground; the brushwork is loose and painterly.

Keep everything else exactly where it is and as it is: the camera and its perspective; the great broken stump at the upper left; the high root mound left of the middle; the low mossy island in the middle; the open water at the lower left and its lily pads; the pools and the hummocks in their places; the muddy near bank at the bottom; the colossal cypresses and their hanging moss; the great tree, its mossy branch, its roots and its bracket fungi at the right edge and in the lower right corner; the mist; the dusk sky and its glow; the colours and the darkness of the picture.

No buildings, no planks, no posts, no fences, no lanterns, no fires, no smoke. No text, no lettering, no signature, no people, no animals.
