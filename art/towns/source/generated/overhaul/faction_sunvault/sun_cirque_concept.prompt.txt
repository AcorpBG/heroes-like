Paint the town screen background of an original fantasy strategy game, in the spirit of classic hand-painted 1990s strategy-game town screens but entirely original: Prismhearth, the home town of the Sunvault Compact, crystal-array commanders who rule by solar crystals, lens relays and harmonic command. One coherent matte painting with dramatic depth and volume, on a bright, cold, clear morning.

The place: a high mountain cirque, wild and steep. A wall of jagged snow peaks and a blue glacier stand behind it; bare golden-grey rock drops from them in broken shelves, ledges, spurs and crags, with patches of snow in the hollows and a few dark dwarf pines, down to a deep turquoise glacial lake at the lower left with a few floes of ice. A thin white meltwater fall runs down the middle of the cirque wall into the lake. This is a mountain, not a desert and not a city: no plain, no paved plazas, no canals, no reflecting pools, no walls or streets between the buildings.

Camera and depth: seen from high on the rim of the cirque, looking across it and down, about 30 degrees down, with strong perspective and great height. Nothing lies on one level. Buildings near the bottom of the picture are large, crisp and saturated, and those at the frame edges may be partly cut off; buildings high on the cirque wall get much smaller and paler, some only bright silhouettes against the snow and the haze. Where one stands in front of another it overlaps it.

Each building stands where its block is in image 1, on its own rock shelf, spur or crag at its own height, joined by narrow stairs cut into the rock and light bridges. The town looks grown, not planned: no rows, no terraces, no grid, no even spacing, and no buildings other than these.

Buildings, by their place in image 1: the large domed hall with a crystal spire on the promontory that juts into the lake is the sun vault, a hall of pale gold stone with a great golden dome and a tall prismatic crystal spire that catches the sun, the focal point; the tall tower at the upper left is a prism spire, a slender tower crowned with a great faceted crystal; the rampart at the upper right closes the mountain pass with three towers tipped with crystals; the ring at the far right is an open calibration court, a round colonnade around a great brass armillary of rings; the three stalls at the left by the lake are a market with white-and-gold awnings; the glass dome at the right is a lens conservatory, a greenhouse of gold ribs and glass where great lenses are grown; the dock at the bottom, on the near shore of the lake, is the shipyard, a stone slip with a gilded lake barge on the stocks and a crane tipped with a lens, large in the foreground; the tall figure at the right edge is a foundry cradle where a colossal construct of gold and white stone with a blazing crystal heart stands as tall as a tower, the largest thing in the foreground. The others are halls, lodges, barracks, workshops and observatories of the same town, each different in shape, of pale gold stone with gilded roofs, domes and crystal finials. Each building is turned at its own angle, showing two sides, with a clear silhouette.

Light: the sun is high at the upper left in a deep blue sky with a few thin clouds; sunlit faces of rock and gold stone are bright and warm, shadows are cool blue and clear, the snow is dazzling. Crystals flash with rainbow glints and a few thin beams of light pass between the spires. The lake is turquoise with bright glints and soft reflections.

Paint the rock, the snow and the water in broad, soft, varied masses with loose painterly brushwork, never as fine parallel strokes or hatching.

Frame: the top 14% is sky and peaks. The lower right corner is plain foreground rock, low and unobtrusive, with nothing tall standing on it.

Materials: pale gold sandstone, gilded metal, white marble, clear and prismatic crystal in blue, violet and rose, brass instruments, glass; precise, luminous and disciplined, not ornate, not a fairy-tale palace.

Style: rich hand-painted digital matte painting with visible brush texture and shapes that read at small size. No outlines, no cel shading, no isometric game-icon look, not a map or a diorama, not photoreal. No text, no lettering, no signature, no interface elements, no people in the foreground.
