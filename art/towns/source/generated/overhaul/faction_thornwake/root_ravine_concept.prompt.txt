Paint the town screen background of an original fantasy strategy game, in the spirit of classic hand-painted 1990s strategy-game town screens but entirely original: Graftroot, the home town of the Thornwake Concord, keepers of migratory living orchards who rule by root law, graft rites and bramble tolls. One coherent matte painting with dramatic depth and volume, on a warm late-spring afternoon.

The place: a deep, steep ravine in an ancient forest of colossal trees, wild and rugged. The left side of the picture is a towering wall of giant roots, mossy rock and root cliffs taller than any building, dropping in broken ledges and buttresses from the upper left toward the lower right; root arches, overhangs, small waterfalls of spring water and hollows break it up. In the middle the mother tree, a trunk as wide as a castle, stands on a high spur of roots and rock that juts out over the ravine; its crown is far above the picture. Far below at the lower right a slow, clear, green woodland river comes round a bend from the far right and widens toward the viewer, with a huge mossy fallen trunk lying across it in the distance. Ferns, wildflowers, mushrooms and small orchard trees in white and pink blossom cling to the ledges. In the lower left corner the viewer looks down onto the crowns of blossoming orchard trees. Far beyond, pale silhouettes of more colossal trunks fade into a soft green-gold haze, with a bright gap of sky between them. This is a forest ravine, not a rolling hillside, not a swamp, not a village green and not a park: no pools, no lawns, no gentle slopes, no paved paths, no fences between the buildings, no rope bridges between the trees.

Camera and depth: seen from a very high bough on the ravine's rim, looking across it and steeply down, about 40 degrees down, with strong perspective and great height: the river lies far below the mother tree's spur, and the upper ledges lie far above it. Nothing lies on one level and no two buildings share a ledge. Buildings near the bottom of the picture are large, crisp and saturated, and those at the frame edges may be partly cut off; buildings high and far up the ravine get much smaller and paler, some only soft shapes in the haze. Where one stands in front of another it overlaps it. Large shapes of light and shadow: a great pool of warm sunlight falls on the mother tree's spur and the river, while the root wall at the left lies mostly in deep cool green shade with sunlit edges.

Each building stands where its block is in image 1, on its own root ledge, buttress, spur or hollow at its own height, joined by narrow mossy root stairs that climb steeply. The town looks grown, not planned: no rows, no grid, no even spacing, and no buildings other than these.

Buildings, by their place in image 1: the great hall with green glass domes that wraps the foot of the mother tree is the concord seat, a hall grown of living wood and woven roots with three domes of green-gold seedglass and warm lit arched doors, the focal point; the tall tower at the upper left is the pollen spire, a slender tower of woven living wood crowned with a great bulb of seedglass; the spiked wall with a gate, far away at the upper right, is a wall of coppiced bramble with a living gate; the dome at the right of the middle and the dome above the river are glasshouses of green-gold seedglass on woven wood, a hothouse and a nursery; the three stalls at the left are a market under mushroom-cap and leaf awnings; the rings are open courts ringed with woven living fences and blossom trees; the great arch right of the middle is a colossal gate of living roots in which a giant construct of bark, root and moss with an amber seed-heart stands as tall as a tower, the largest thing in the foreground; the dock at the bottom right, on the near bank of the river, is the shipyard, a boat cradle of living roots with a broad river barge with a leaf-shaped sail on the stocks and a boat shed roofed with bark and moss, large in the foreground. The others are lodges, halls, hothouses and workshops of the same town, each different in shape: grown of living wood and woven branches, with roofs of bark, moss and leaves, some with small seedglass domes. Each building is turned at its own angle, showing two sides, with a clear silhouette.

Light: warm afternoon sun comes down through the canopy gap from the upper right; the mother tree's roots, the terraces and the river are dappled with green-gold light and soft leaf shadow, shadows are deep green and cool. Lanterns of amber glass glow in doorways and under eaves. There are no distinct beams or rays of light, only a soft glow in the haze.

Paint the moss, the ferns, the bark and the blossom in broad, soft, varied masses with loose painterly brushwork, never as fine parallel strokes or hatching.

Frame: the top 14% is canopy, far trunks and the bright gap. The lower left corner is only blossom crowns, with no building on them. Nothing stands in front of the buildings: no dark branches, leaves or trunks frame the picture in the foreground.

Materials: living wood, woven branches and roots, bark, moss, bramble, green-gold seedglass, amber lanterns, white and pink blossom; grown, patient and watchful, not a fairy village, not cute, not elven palaces.

Style: rich hand-painted digital matte painting with visible brush texture and shapes that read at small size. No outlines, no cel shading, no isometric game-icon look, not a map or a diorama, not photoreal. No text, no lettering, no signature, no interface elements, no people in the foreground.
