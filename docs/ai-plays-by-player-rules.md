# AI Plays By Player Rules

Owner feedback, 2026-10-07: enemy "raids" feel wrong; the AI should play like a human with real heroes that take mines, improve towns and fight map guards under the same rules as the player.

## Model before this change

- `EnemyTurnRules.run_enemy_turn` ran an "empire cycle" per computer player: income, weekly musters, town builds (with an AI-only pacing floor), a hero task board, raid reinforcement, abstract pressure gain, raid launches and an abstract siege counter.
- Enemy armies on the map were raid encounters (`session.overworld.encounters` entries with `spawned_by_faction_id`), spawned at towns once a pressure counter crossed a threshold. Their armies came from encounter templates such as "Mireclaw Raid", led by a commander from the faction roster.
- `EnemyAdventureRules.advance_raids` moved each raid one tile per day, chose targets through a large planner, "broke" neutral and dwelling guards by comparing strength numbers without losses, pillaged the player's treasury on arrival, and could capture towns through a siege counter.

## Model now (`scripts/core/AiHeroRules.gd`)

`EnemyTurnRules.run_enemy_turn` calls `AiHeroRules.run_faction_turn` for every computer player. The raid director is removed (2026-10-07). What is left in `EnemyAdventureRules` serves heroes and old saves: commander roster records, raid army normalization for the save migration, the site seizure `_secure_resource_target` and the AI event records. Its multi-level path surfaces are not used in play; the RMG transit and start placement validation tools plan journeys with them. `EnemyTurnRules` keeps the turn loop, enemy state normalization and the threat summaries.

### Heroes

- An AI hero is an encounter entry (so map rendering, fog, battles against the human, saves and turn playback keep working) with an `ai_hero` block: hero id, movement `{current, max}`, home town, hire day, private goal and a short avoid list. Its hero record is `enemy_commander_state`; its army is `enemy_army`, made of real troops. The encounter template (`encounter_mire_raid`) only supplies battle settings; battles are called "Battle with <hero>".
- Every computer player starts with one hero and its faction's starting muster, like the human. On generated maps every faction's starting muster rates about 140 (`army_start_<faction>` in `content/army_groups.json`, `NativeRandomMapPackageSessionBridge.GENERATED_START_ARMY_BY_FACTION`; owner decision 2026-10-08): the earlier default armies ran from 90 (Embercourt) to 169 (Veilmourn), and in the series-20261008 matches Veilmourn won 4 of 4. Town starting garrisons still differ by faction. More heroes are hired in a town with a Tavern through the shared hire path (`HeroCommandRules.tavern_offer` + `build_recruited_hero`), paying the template's gold cost. Wanted heroes (`_desired_hero_count`, `_govern_towns`): 1 + towns held, plus one from day 14 (`EXTRA_HERO_DAY`), then one explorer per 1,500 unexplored land tiles (`TILES_PER_EXPLORER`), at most one per town held: the count grows with the towns, with no cap of its own (owner, 2026-10-28; from 2026-10-26 it was 8 in all). Every hire must leave gold in the treasury: two days of the player's gold income for a town hero (`HIRE_RESERVE_DAYS`), five for an explorer (`EXPLORER_RESERVE_DAYS`), so a player without an economy does not hire explorers. A player still short of town heroes keeps the next hire's gold and that reserve back from what its towns spend the same day (`_govern_towns`, 2026-10-28): its towns bought troops with every coin first, so in ai-match-3232670313 the eventual winner held 7 to 13 towns with 2 to 4 heroes from day 42 to day 140, hired none, and its garrisons grew to 110,000 against 6,000 in the field. A player hires from the pair each of its towns' taverns offers that week, as the human does: heroes of every faction, the town's own faction about four times in five (owner, 2026-10-28). It tries the town with the strongest garrison first, so the new hero can take those troops along. Until 2026-10-28 every player hired only its own faction's 11 heroes (`content/heroes.json`) from one pair for all its towns, so no player led more than 11. The starting hero is still of the player's own faction. Until 2026-10-26 a player wanted at most 1 + min(towns, 2) + 1 heroes, and the human and computer players alike were held to four heroes.
- Movement uses the human's rules: `movement_max_for_hero` at normal difficulty, one point per tile, full budget each day. Pathing is an 8-way search with the overworld corner rule; water and rock are impassable, object bodies block, town doorways and site visit tiles can be stood on. Guard zones, armies, heroes and the tiles around hostile heroes end a route; the player's own heroes inside one of its towns (on the doorway) stand in no one's way. A site that holds nothing more for the hero (a picked-up cache, its own mine) can be walked across, and so can the doorway of a building that stands after a visit (shrines, schools, roadside producers, signs; `walk_across` in `core/port/ai_hero_rules_knowledge.cpp`): in the owner's 2026-10-08 game Embercourt's main hero entered a pocket whose only way out ran over a shrine's doorway and could not plan its way back. A one-time site that stays standing once looted (a guarded bank) holds nothing for anyone and is walked across by every player, not only by the one who looted it: whoever loots a site does not own it. Until 2026-10-17 a looted Amber Reliquary in the only passage between two players on a small map (seed ai-match-2298949441) let the looter's heroes through and stopped the other's main army, 4,600 strong, for 250 days; the game now ends on day 61.
- Knowledge is fog-honest. Each computer player keeps an explored map (`ai_explored`, base64 bitsets per level). Its towns see as far as the human's (`OverworldRules.TOWN_VISION_RADIUS`), its heroes their scouting radius along every step. Map objects count once explored; human heroes and rival AI heroes only while in sight, and a player remembers those it has seen for 28 days (see Finding the enemy). A hostile hero that takes one of its towns counts as seen at the town's doorway that day, and for three days a hero seen counts as still about where it was seen (see Danger). With nothing worth doing in reach, a hero walks to the nearest unexplored frontier.

### Choosing what to do

Each decision is a utility score: value / (1 + distance / daily movement), with a bonus for keeping yesterday's goal. Values are gold equivalents.

- Resource pickups, mines and other claimable sites (resources the faction is short of count 1.8x), dwellings (a week of troops) and visit buildings (what the building gives, in gold, only when the hero has the level and the faction the price it asks).
- Artifacts.
- Neutral guards and wandering armies the hero beats with a 1.3x power margin (1.5x before the strength estimate was recalibrated on 2026-10-08). A guard is worth its reward, plus what stands in its zone (sites, artifacts, a town doorway: on generated maps guards sit next to what they protect without a link), plus the ground it opens: open ground behind its zone the hero cannot reach today counts 300 and 4 for each unexplored tile, and sites already seen there count at half their value (up to 5000 in all). From that come the troops the fight should cost: Lanchester's square law (a defender r times the hero's strength costs 1 - sqrt(1 - r^2) of it) raised to what auto-resolve costs, 1.3 times the square law plus 1.2% (calibrated on 1,262 auto-resolved fights of the three computer players' main heroes against the guards of the owner's 2026-10-08 map: mean losses 8.7% at a guard of 0.3-0.4 of the hero's strength, 14% at 0.4-0.5, 22% at 0.5-0.6 and 32% at 0.6-0.7, about a third above the square law). A guard's reward is what the battle will pay for it (see Fights). Before setting out to fight a guard or a rival hero the hero runs the same auto-resolve on a scratch session (skipped at a 3x margin, unless the rival hero's strongest damage spell it has mana for would take a quarter or more of the army's health in one cast), and goes only when it wins, keeps at least half its strength and the fight still pays with the losses that battle really shows; otherwise it leaves that target for three days.
- Towns (power margin 1.4x over the garrison plus any hostile hero standing in the doorway), human heroes and other computer players' heroes in sight (1.3x). A hero out of sight stays a target for three days after it was last seen, as a human follows a raider's fresh tracks (`_recently_seen`); after that, only where its zone stands first on the way to a town this player knows and could take (`_war_paths`, `_hero_war_value`): reaching that town means fighting it there. A town is worth what holding it gives (`_town_worth`): 10,000 plus four weeks of its income and of the troops its dwellings muster, priced as the town planner prices them, 2,000 more for the human's; a player that has lost every town values one twice as much. Knocking a player out adds `ELIMINATION_VALUE` (20,000) shared among what it still holds: each of its towns carries 20,000 divided by the number it holds, and once it holds none each of its heroes carries 20,000 divided by the number of its heroes. Until 2026-10-09 a town was worth 4,000 plus two weeks with no elimination value; on a medium map (seed ai-match-3624800192) neutral towns with 24 defenders stood untaken for 40 to 70 days and a beaten player kept its last town for 40 days against an army twenty times its own. With the new values the same match ends on Day 85 instead of 99. Until 2026-10-08 every town was worth a flat 4,000 (6,000 the human's), about what a mine is, so a hero that had lost its capital flagged mines for ten days with the empty capital ten tiles away. A rival hero is worth 1,200 plus half its army, plus half the worth of every town of ours it could take within two days, on either level (`_threatened_town_share`): a game is won by taking a player's towns and killing its heroes, and a raider loose among our towns costs all of them (owner, 2026-10-28; until then only the most valuable town on its own level within a day counted). Town assaults and dwelling guards subtract the same loss estimate.
- Its own towns: the main hero collects the garrison, a lesser hero picks it up to carry it out when the main hero is more than a day's march away, and a hostile hero near the town makes it urgent. A visit is worth only what the hero can take along (`_garrison_pickup`): every stack that merges or fits, then the garrison's stronger stacks in place of the hero's weakest, as a human arranges seven slots. A full army used to walk back for garrison troops of other kinds it could not take, every day. An own town holding troops counts however far it lies, on this level or past a gate, and a lesser hero carrying its troops out is paid what they are worth (2026-10-29; see Long-range planning). To the main and field armies the troops are also worth what the bigger army can then take: the best rival or neutral town it knows, or rival hero in sight, within twelve days that it cannot beat now and can with them, less the expected losses, per day of travel from the town (`_newly_beatable_value`, 2026-10-29).
- Its own main and field armies: a lesser hero walks over and hands its troops to the one that gains most. The two armies are pooled, the receiver keeps the strongest seven stacks and the courier the rest, at least one creature (`_meet_candidate`, `_meet_hero`); a hand-over is worth what the receiver gains, so a courier bringing a rear town's garrison goes before small errands. Faction plays no part beyond the slots: a creature of another faction fills a slot as well as one of the army's own. Until 2026-10-29 only the main hero took hand-overs, though in big games most heroes are field armies (a player holding 21 towns has 20), and before that day a hand-over and a courier's pickup were worth at most what a modest site is worth (600), so the troops waited while lesser heroes finished their own errands.
- On the way: a hero heading for a hand-over or for an own town's troops first takes a site, an artifact or a guard just off its way when doing that first is worth more per day than going straight, the two values over the days of the detour and the rest of the way against the errand's alone (`_on_the_way`). Only what lies nearer than the errand counts, and a fight only when its preview looks good. Until 2026-10-29 such a hero took only fights that cost it nothing on the way.

Field armies. A player holding more than one town keeps lesser heroes as field armies, one per town beyond the first (at most three until 2026-10-26), the most experienced first (`_field_hero_ids`). The roles are given out at the start of the player's day (`_assign_field_heroes`): the field armies keep their places, and a hero keeps the role a week after the towns that called for it are lost. Until 2026-10-29 the roles were counted again at every decision, and towns change hands daily late in a big game: in ai-match-1324051695 the leader held 11 to 13 towns from one day to the next, and over days 205 to 220 ten of its 21 heroes changed role 35 times, each change sending the hero after another errand, often through a gate and back. A field hero picks up garrisons and takes couriers' troops as the main hero does, does not hand its troops to the main hero except to join it when the main army alone cannot beat a hostile hero or town it knows of (the main hero then takes the stronger stacks), holds a threatened town's gate when that deters the attack, and otherwise takes what it can beat with its own army. Captured towns of other factions muster creatures the main army has no slot for; before 2026-10-09 those piled up in garrisons and every lesser hero was a courier with one creature. In the medium-map match above the winner ended with three armies of about 2,200 instead of one.

Leaving a defence. A hero leaving one of its towns weighs keeping troops there (`_leave_defense`): enough to stop the strongest visible hostile hero within two days' march on either level (it would need 1.4x the garrison). A town no hero threatens keeps nothing: its troops travel with the heroes, which deal with the threats (owner, 2026-10-28; until then it kept a raider of 40 strength per week of the game when a known rival town lay within 24 tiles). It leaves the weakest creatures that cover that, but only while they are worth less than the town and at most 30% of the main hero's army (50% of a field army's); otherwise taking the whole garrison is the better trade. A rival that holds no town musters nothing and is out of the game after seven days unless it takes a town back, so every town is also held against the strongest hero of such a rival this player remembers, wherever it was seen (`_townless_raider`). Until 2026-10-20 a townless rival counted only when seen near, and its heroes kept it in the game by walking into the towns the leader's armies had emptied: in a four-player large game on two levels (seed ai-match-4265064792) the last rival held no town from day 247, and its heroes of about 1,000 strength took the leader's towns 15 times until day 276, 11 of them empty. Switched on at day 246 the rule ends that game on day 259, with 4 captures after day 246 instead of 24; played with it from day 1 the game ends on day 217 after 308 captures (107 of empty towns) instead of 464 (201). After that day's Sunvault trim the same settings give other games: ai-match-737604772 ends on day 160 instead of 262 (121 captures instead of 313), while ai-match-4265064792 and ai-match-3080264325 run to the 320-day cap with or without the rule (see Known gaps). A town draws a hero back for troops only for what the hero would keep. Field armies: one per town beyond the first.

Finding the enemy. Heroes only target towns they have seen. A town counts as seen once its anchor tile or its doorway is explored, and every check uses that one rule (`_town_seen`); until 2026-10-17 scouting tested the anchor and the planners the doorway, so on a 6-player large map (seed ai-match-1305188145) the last rival town, its anchor seen and its door not, was neither scouted for nor attacked and stood 280 days next to a 320,000 army. While a rival still holds a town this player has never seen, the nearest unexplored ground is worth up to 20,000 more than the usual 500, rising from day 21 to day 56 (`_explore_value`). From day 56 the usual 500 grows with the day, toward the endgame: 1,000 on day 112, 2,000 on day 224 (owner, 2026-10-28). In a four-player medium game (seed ai-match-3726749296) the leader, 32,000 strong, never scouted the two corners where its rivals each held one town guarded by under 100, and the game ran to its 160-day cap; with the hunt it ends on day 112. A Chartmaker (1,000 gold, the whole level, once per player) is worth what it shows: that value of unexplored ground times the share of the level's open ground still unexplored (`_chart_value`), less half its price as for every building; a visit marks the whole level explored (2026-10-29). Until then its reveal counted nothing, its price put it below zero, so no hero went, and a visit would have shown the AI nothing.

A player far ahead hunts harder. It remembers every hostile hero it has seen in the last 28 days and that hero's strength then (`ai_sightings` in its state; a hero it beats is forgotten). Once it has found a rival's town and its main army is more than 1.3 times all it knows of any one rival together, the heroes it remembers and the garrisons of that rival's towns it has seen (`_far_ahead`), unexplored ground is worth as much more as one of its own towns is worth on average, at least 20,000 (`_hunt_value`). Before this (2026-10-20) a town was worth about 275,000 late in a large game and scouting 20,500, so a leader whose rival's raiders kept taking its towns kept retaking them and never looked for the rival's other towns. In a four-player large game on two levels (seed ai-match-3844121282) the leader's 150,000-strength main army retook towns taken by raiders of 5,000 to 17,000. The contested towns lay on both levels, so each retake crossed gates and the raiders took back the towns on the level it had left. Garrisons stayed at one week's muster (about 1,900) or nothing, because gold buys only what a town musters, and the raiders kept out of the main army's reach. The game made 203 captures from day 200 to its 320-day cap, 111 of them of empty towns, and the leader never saw the rival's other four towns. With the hunt it ends on day 198 after 139 captures (47 of empty towns) instead of 369 (182); on the same kind of map seed ai-match-737604772 ends on day 147 instead of 311 and ai-match-3080264325 on day 205 instead of 290. In ai-match-4265064792 the hunt barely changes the game (day 276, 274 before): the leader had not seen two of the last rival's towns until about day 229, and until day 222 its main army of 4,000 to 13,000 was not 1.3 times another rival's raiders of 10,000 to 17,000, while 55,000 to 190,000 stood in its garrisons, mostly other factions' creatures its main army had no slot for. Counting those garrisons towards being far ahead, or drawing the main army home for the troops it lacked, was tried and dropped (2026-10-20): both change the game from day 16 to 43, when a player knows little of its rivals, and in all six variants ai-match-737604772 and ai-match-3080264325 ended 11 to 173 days later, three games running to the 320-day cap. That game's last 30 days were the last rival's heroes, holding no town, walking into mostly empty towns (see Leaving a defence). Garrisons sized against the remembered raiders were tried and dropped: raiders grow after they are seen and troops left in a town fight without the hero's attack and defence, so the main army fell from 169,000 to 20,000 in three weeks.

Portals. The route search goes through portals on the level: the hero comes out on an exit, one movement point later. A fixed portal (a Wayring pair, a one-way gate with a single exit) leads to its exit. A network with several exits sends the traveller to a random one, rolled with the human's rule (`OverworldRules.native_random_exit_check`); the AI plans with the exits of that network it has already seen, which the network colour tells apart on the map, and plans again from wherever it comes out. A reachable portal with an exit the player has not seen counts as unexplored ground: a hero scouting it steps through. That includes an exit on the other level: a network whose exits lie on both levels sends a hero that steps in to either, rolled as for the human, and the hero plans again on whichever level it comes out (2026-10-29). Until then the AI kept only the exits on the portal's own level, so such a network looked like a fixed portal to that exit and its heroes walked through it there without the roll. On a medium map whose halves met only at a Wayring pair, two players never met before fixed portals were used, and on a large map a player whose start region opened only through random networks was out of everyone's reach before those were (2026-10-09).

Levels. The route search covers the hero's level; subterranean gates connect the two (`_gate_candidate`), and so does a monolith whose one exit is on the other level, which the AI plans and crosses exactly as a gate. Until 2026-10-17 heroes never used such a monolith: on two-level maps whose start region opened only through one, its player stayed shut in and no rival came (seeds `ai-match-1520175333` and `ai-match-113824639` ran to the 320-day cap). A random network with exits on the other level is a gate too, one that comes out there one try in its number of exits (2026-10-29): the ways through several gates (`_gate_routes`) count each miss as a day's walk back to it, nothing beyond it counts on its own, crossing rolls the exit as for the human (`native_random_exit_check`, which passes over guarded or taken exits), and a hero that comes out on its own level keeps its errand and tries again. The way home (`_gate_home`) takes only fixed gates. In the 6-player extra large two-level game ai-match-2174623860 (batch of 2026-10-29) the last rival's half of the map, on both levels, opened only through two such networks, each with one exit in that half and one on the leader's own ground. The leader's 15 heroes had explored everything else by day 140 and the game reached the 320-day cap with the rival's towns there unseen; no hero of any player planned a way through those networks or went to one of the map's six Chartmakers. With the change all six players plan through the networks and buy charts (days 32 to 161), and the game is decided on day 224. For each of the three nearest gates it can walk to, the hero looks at the other level as if it stood at the gate's exit, takes the best candidate there by the usual rules (fog-honest, so unexplored ground is worth scouting) and scores it by the whole way. Generated maps guard a gate at both ends. A guard whose zone covers the gate on this side is fought first, valued as a guard fight plus what lies beyond; the guard at the far end is fought on arrival, as the human fights it (`native_passage_travel_check` with `fight_on_arrival`), so the hero must be able to beat it and its expected losses count. Whichever fight comes next is previewed. Main and field armies weigh the gates against their near and strategic choices every decision; couriers do for an own town holding troops or a main or field army beyond (2026-10-29), and look through a gate for anything else when nothing on their level is worth doing. Past the three days around the exit only what is worth the walk counts: rival and neutral towns and hostile heroes for main and field armies, own towns holding troops and the main and field armies for every hero (until 2026-10-29 own towns past a gate counted only within those three days). Ways through several gates (`_gate_routes`) lead the main and field armies to rival and neutral towns and unseen ground, and every hero to its own towns holding troops (2026-10-29; until then no hero planned a way to an own town, so a town in a pocket two gates away waited for a hero that happened to pass the first gate). A hero that crosses keeps the errand it crossed for as its goal, and so does one walking to a gate: its goal is what lies beyond, whichever gate leads there, and the ways through several gates keep that errand against a slightly better one (`GOAL_CONTINUITY_BONUS`). For three days after crossing it takes another gate only toward that errand (`kGateCommitDays`), unless nothing on its level is worth doing; a hostile hero beyond a gate counts only for a hero that reaches it the day it crosses (2026-10-29). What lies beyond changes faster than a hero walks there: a garrison another of the player's heroes empties first, a rival hero that moves off when the army comes through, a threat to a town that passes. Re-weighing every day, heroes crossed back and forth after them. In eight extra large two-level replays (the seeds under Long-range planning) half of all crossings went back the other way within three days, 3,015 of 6,013 (496 by main armies). In ai-match-3232670313 the second player's main army crossed gates 3950 and 3949 every day from day 171 to 178 after a raider of a third its strength that walked off whenever the army came through: from the far side it was worth 636,000 and in reach, on its own side gone. In ai-match-1979409395 a lesser hero went round four gates every two days from day 221 for own towns' troops another hero took first and a threatened town three days away. With the change 1,419 of 5,188 crossings go back within three days (250 by main armies), and runs of three or more days changing level fall from 689 to 133 hero-days. Until 2026-10-17 heroes never left their level: in the 2026-10-16 batch 22 of 32 two-level games ran to the 160-day cap (6 of 60 one-level land games), main armies idle with nothing left on their level while the rivals beyond the gates kept their towns. An idle hero whose player holds no town on its level heads home through the gate with the shortest way to the town's doorway (`_gate_home`); until 2026-10-17 it waited on its own level at the town's coordinates.

Water. The route search sails as the human does (`docs/water-travel.md`): a walking hero steps onto a free boat, or stands at a shipyard and has a boat built at its dock for the human's price (1,000 gold, 10 wood, from the player's treasury), then sails over open water, never through another boat, and steps ashore onto open ground or a doorway, leaving the boat moored for anyone to take. Boarding and going ashore each end the day, so a route through either counts half a day's movement more, and a boat built at a shipyard one day more; a route boards once. Everything across the water is then weighed by the usual rules, less the boat's price (1,800 in gold-equivalent) where the route buys one, so a hero does not buy a boat to scout or for a small pickup. Towns spend before heroes move, so on islands maps the treasury seldom held 1,000 gold and 10 wood: a main or field army with nothing to do and a shipyard in reach marks the day (`ai_boat_wanted_day`), and for two days the towns keep the boat's price back. A boat may be paid after trades at one of the player's markets, as the human trades from any of its towns. A hero aboard carries `boat_id` and the boat's `hero_id` is the hero's placement id; a hero defeated aboard leaves the boat empty where it lay. Shipyards and whirlpools are not errands of their own. Until 2026-10-17 water stayed impassable for computer heroes, so islands maps left every player on its own island (the 2026-10-16 batch left them out for that reason); replays on four islands maps (small and medium, two and four players) now each end with a winner, on days 25 to 111.

Concentration. A field army joins the main army, which takes the stronger stacks, when the main army cannot beat a hostile hero in sight or seen in the last three days, a known town, or the guard the main army last found blocking its way (`BLOCKED_NEED_KEY`): the first guard on the way to a known rival town or, with the rivals unseen and no ground left to scout, the weakest guard around it cannot beat. The decision log records that need as `blocked_need`. It also joins when the main army's battle preview lost to a rival hero in sight or seen in the last three days, or kept too little, though the strength estimate rates that hero beatable (`_note_vetoed_need`, the main hero's `vetoed_needs`): that hero then needs the main army's power plus what the preview leaves it, times `HERO_ATTACK_RATIO`, until a preview looks good (2026-10-28). In m566 of the owner's 2026-10-28 mixed batch (ai-match-3142897504, extra large 6-player, one level) the estimate put the main army at 37,800 against a lone raider of 25,200 carrying 681 shooters from captured towns; the preview lost on days 307, 312 and 316, the raider beat 16 field heroes of 2,000 to 15,000 one by one, and the game reached the cap. With the rule the field armies bring the main army to 53,600 by day 312, it beats the raider on day 313 and the game ends on day 317; the six capped seeds below play exactly as without it. Otherwise it keeps its army and works on its own: seven stacks limit an army, not a share of the main army's strength (owner, 2026-10-28; until then a field army above 35% of the main army's strength went and joined it, `FIELD_SHARE_MAX`). In the owner's 2026-10-28 batch (600 extra large 6-player two-level games, strong guards) 76 games reached the 320-day cap, 28 of the 68 on maps of 24 or more towns: from the day the leader held half the towns it took about 92 towns per 100 days and lost 86, 80% of them within 7 days of taking them, mostly to raiders a third as strong as its main army that its field armies could have beaten. Six of those capped games replayed with the 2026-10-28 rules (heroes with the towns, the hire hold-back, a hostile hero worth every town it threatens on either level, no baseline garrison, no strength-share merge, scouting worth more late) all end, on days 210 to 278, with 250 to 488 captures instead of 508 to 810 (ai-match-1979409395, -23293556, -3672386705, -1324051695, -2813289413, -3232670313). With the taverns of every faction added the same day (see Heroes), the six end on days 207 to 320 (259, 239, 300, 320, 207, 246 against 228, 210, 266, 227, 211, 278), with 292 to 607 captures, 1,004 of them of undefended towns. The leaders now lead 28 to 43 heroes at once, and 12% of all the heroes hired in the first three weeks come from another faction; later most of a leader's hires do, from the captured towns. In the last four-week sample each leader keeps 20 to 37% of its strength in garrisons. Six seeds are too few to say whether the later ends are a trend. While a rival's towns are unseen, scouting is worth up to 20,000 (`HUNT_EXPLORE_VALUE`, from day 21 to day 56), and up to what one of its towns is worth for a player far ahead (see Finding the enemy). On the stalled 4-player large-map seed (ai-match-557313547) the game now ends on day 159. A recheck on 2026-10-28 (36 games on d95171685: 24 large 4-player and 12 extra large 6-player maps, normal and strong guards) ended every game, on days 91 to 246, none at the cap. A player far ahead still finishes slowly: from the day it holds half the towns, a median of 60 days on large maps and 96 on extra large ones, seven games over 120. Meanwhile it keeps on average 33% (large) and 41% (extra large) of its strength in garrisons, over half in five games, and its strongest hero holds 43 to 48% of its field strength across 5 to 7 heroes, as the 35% cap allows. In ai-match-1733907118 (extra large, normal guards) the leader holds 8 to 12 of the 14 towns from day 143, with 20,000 to 59,000 in garrisons and heroes of 5,000 to 10,000; the rival's lone 33,000 hero retakes towns, and only on day 239 does the leader field a 54,000 army. The game ends on day 246.

Long-range planning. Each decision searches three days of movement around the hero. The main hero and the field armies also search twelve days out (`_strategic_candidate`) for what decides the game: known rival and neutral towns, hostile heroes, the guards standing first in the way to a known town and, while a rival's towns are unseen, the nearest unexplored ground; the best of these competes with the best local choice by the same value per day of travel, so a town worth a hundred mines across the map wins over the mine next door. Before this (2026-10-09) the leading players on large maps found a mine or a resource pile within three days every day and never went for the rival towns in the far corners; four of ten large-map games ran to the day cap. The same search weighs the player's own towns holding troops and the main army, and couriers run it for those alone (2026-10-29): troops travel with the heroes, not in unthreatened garrisons (owner, 2026-10-28). Until then it skipped own towns and couriers searched three days: in extra large 6-player games of the owner's 2026-10-28 batches the garrisons held 35 to 50% of all players' strength over days 100 to 200, and 16 to 34% sat in towns no hero had emptied for four weeks or more, piles of 22,000 to 45,000 untouched for 40 to 140 days, 23 to 73 tiles from the nearest hero or in a pocket reached only through a gate. Replayed with the change, with the field roles held for a week (see Field armies), eight of those games (ai-match-1979409395, -23293556, -3672386705, -1324051695, -2813289413, -3232670313, -3142897504, -3008409200) keep 27 to 42% of the strength in garrisons and 11 to 23% in such piles, and all end, on days 226, 247, 243, 172, 238, 254, 118 and 161, against 201, the cap, 273, 208, the cap, the cap, 317 and 189. With the hand-overs to field armies, the detours on the way, the pickups weighed by what they let an army take and the ways through several gates to own towns (2026-10-29), the same eight keep 8 to 32% of the strength in garrisons over days 100 to 200 (12 to 42% before) and 1 to 17% in such piles (1 to 23%; 23 to 5% in ai-match-23293556), with turn times per day 23% lower to 33% higher, and end on days 273, 194, 227, 314, 173, 210, 140 and 178 against 226, 247, 243, 172, 238, 254, 118 and 161. A build of the same rules a step earlier ended them on other days, two at the cap (one of them the island below, see Known gaps): eight seeds say nothing about game length. Detours are short: in ai-match-23293556 the median detour lay 10 tiles away on a 30-tile errand.

Guards in the way. A guard's fight is also worth half of the most valuable known town out of reach whose shortest way, walking through guard zones, enters this guard's zone first (`_war_paths`), and, while a rival's towns are still unseen, unexplored ground behind a guard is worth up to half of `ELIMINATION_VALUE`.

Lesser heroes act before the main hero each day, so troops they hand over are with the main hero when it acts. The most experienced hero among those holding at least half the strongest hero's army (the strongest army among equals) leads the main army. Going by army alone, a lesser hero carrying a big garrison out to the main hero became the main hero itself; going by experience alone, a veteran whose army had been beaten stayed "main" and an 821-strength hero handed its army to a town for it. A lesser hero near the main hero leaves all but its weakest stack in a town for it.

A computer player's hire gets its level-up growth seed from the session (`HeroCommandRules.new_growth_seed`, passed to `build_recruited_hero` before any levels it arrives with), as the human's hires do; without it each AI hero rolled a random seed and the same match seed played out differently (2026-10-08).

Town defense. A town is threatened when a hostile hero (the human's or another computer player's) that this player can see could reach its doorway next turn with more than 1.15x the garrison, on either level: across levels the way runs through the gate or monolith whose exit lies nearest the doorway (until 2026-10-28 a hero on the other level never counted). In a threatened town no hero takes the garrison along and a hero standing in it defends it. The hero and the garrison fight in one battle when their stacks fit its seven slots, stacks of one kind merging if needed (the garrison's into the hero's first stack of that kind; `HeroCommandRules.entry_defense_plan`); the survivors go back to each side in proportion to what it brought (`split_entry_defense`). When even merged they need more than seven, the attacker fights the hero first and the garrison after. This holds for every attacker and defender: a computer player attacking another (`_attack_town`), one attacking the human's town with a hero in it (`town_defense_force`, a town defense battle, or a battle with the hero alone when it does not fit) and the human attacking a computer town held by a hero (`create_entry_defense_assault_payload`). A hero on its own town's doorway is no target of its own: an attack on it is an attack on the town (`_entry_town_index`). Until 2026-10-26 the hero always fought first and alone. The main hero standing in it holds the gate while the threat is too strong to attack; a lesser hero would only be lost there and goes on. A weaker threat the hero can catch today it hunts; one it cannot catch today would walk back in behind it, so the hero leaves just enough creatures of one kind to beat it (at most a quarter of its own strength) and goes about its day, or stays when that would cost more. A hero that takes a town buys the town's available troops into the garrison at once, as a human does. Until 2026-10-08 computer players only saw the human's heroes as threats, and a town could be taken from under a hero standing in it, so in AI-against-AI matches 45 of 48 captures in 40 days met no defenders and a capital changed hands twice a day between couriers. With defense that match is decided on day 13 after one capture, past defenders; the Thornwake-Veilmourn pair goes from 37 and 25 captures to 11 and 3, and an 87-day Mireclaw-Brasshollow war with 69 captures (38 unopposed) to 37 days with 19 (12).

Danger. A visible hostile hero at least 1.15x a hero's strength (`DANGER_RATIO`) is a danger to it (`_dangers`): the hero picks nothing within that hero's next-turn reach plus one tile, except its own town when it and the garrison make the town too strong to storm (1.4x, `_deters`). A hero already within such reach and with nothing safe to do walks to the tile it can reach today farthest outside it (`_evade`). The main hero holds a threatened town's gate only while that deters the attack; against more it would only be lost with the town. On series map 8 (Veilmourn against Mireclaw, capitals 18 tiles apart with an open route) Mireclaw's main hero walked into Veilmourn's far stronger one on day 4 and lost the game on day 5; with even musters and danger avoidance the two games last 13 and 39 days.

A hostile hero out of sight that the player saw in the last three days, its own day included, counts as a danger where it was last seen, with the strength and reach it had then (`_recent_hostiles`, `kRecentSightingDays`), while it is still on the map. Its sightings keep that place and reach (`ai_sightings`: x, y, level, reach), and a computer player that loses a town to a hostile hero, the human's or another computer player's, notes that hero at the town's doorway that day (`note_town_taken`). Until 2026-10-29 only heroes in sight counted, and the main army dropped a raider that left its sight (see Choosing what to do) and called no field army against one it could not see (see Concentration). In m665 of the owner's 2026-10-29 batch (ai-match-928755747, extra large 6-player, two levels) the leader's 60,000 main army went after towns while a 45,000 raider took one of its towns on days 305 and 306, left its sight each time, and killed the leader's heroes of 8,500 to 25,000 one by one as they walked back in (nine from day 268), and the game reached the 320-day cap. Replayed on the batch's maps (48be34427 without and with the change), the nine games of that batch that reached the cap all end: m665, m154, m684, m273, m046, m421, m700, m349 and m283 (ai-match-928755747, -2921888881, -3125731143, -918029868, -1929360114, -2479589970, -770530257, -1038035584, -2174623860) on days 230, 146, 205, 203, 148, 225, 261, 279 and 157, against the cap, 245, the cap, the cap, 130, 187, 200, 307 and 224, with fewer captures in six of them. The games part from day 6 to 62, so nine seeds say little about game length. In m665 the raider falls on day 226 and the leader loses no hero after day 250.

A guard's zone works as it does for the human: anything inside it is reached by fighting the guard first, and rock or an object's body inside the zone cannot be stood on (only a doorway or visit tile there). A fight that leaves the guard standing sends the hero back to the tile it attacked from. A hero hemmed in by zones fights the weakest of those guards when the odds are at least even: when it cannot move at all, or when its search reached every tile it can walk to and none of its towns is among them.

Heroes guard the tiles around them as guards do (owner report 2026-10-09: an enemy hero walked right past the main hero and no battle started). `OverworldRules.hero_guard_zone_tiles` gives a hero's zone: its eight neighbours, only land for a hero on land and only water for one at sea, and none for a hero standing in a town's doorway, whose town defends it. A hero that steps into a hostile hero's zone fights that hero there. For the human, a computer player's hero that is not its ally joins the guard engagement index (`guard_engagement_by_tile`): routes end on its zone and never cross it, selecting the hero walks to the nearest zone tile (`guard_engagement_approach_route`), and arriving there, by foot or through a portal, starts the battle. For a computer player, `_search` marks the zones of the human's heroes and of rival players' heroes as route ends (`hero_zone`), also at portal exits, so a hero goes around another army or fights it: the hostile hero is the only target on a tile in its zone, a town doorway inside one is not a place to park, and a portal inside one is not scouted. The search ends routes on every hostile hero's zone, also one out of sight, so such a hero is a target when its zone stands first on the way to a known town (see Choosing what to do). Until 2026-10-28 only heroes in sight were, and heroes standing before a known town's doorway hid the town: in a six-player extra-large water game (seed ai-match-3008409200, batch of 2026-10-28) the last rival town stood on a 201-tile coast reached only from the sea, three of its heroes stood in front of its doorway, and the leader's routes all ended in their zones. On day 200 every one of the leader's heroes valued the town at about 290,000, and `_war_paths` credited the weakest of those heroes (strength 55) with 146,000 for the way, but none of them was in sight, so the leader picked up resources between its own towns until the 320-day cap. With the change the same match is decided on day 189. A hero whose step still lands in such a zone (a random portal exit) stops there and fights (`_follow_plan`). Portal endpoints next to a hero stay open; only neutral guards close them. Until then a hero blocked only its own tile.

With nothing worth doing, a hero first takes a second look at up to two fights it dismissed on the estimate: the preview shows what each really costs, and one that pays after all, or that it wins keeping three quarters of its strength, is fought (standing still earns nothing).

A hero that still finds nothing searches again as if the player's own heroes were not on the map (`_clear_way`). When something worth doing lies past one of its idle heroes, that hero is asked to step aside, into its town or off the way: at once if it can still move today, otherwise first thing on its next turn (`ai_hero.make_way`).

Otherwise the hero walks back, over as many days as it takes, to its home town and parks inside it (`ai_hero.parked`): one idle hero per town, standing on the doorway, where the player's own heroes are in the town and in no one's way. Other idle heroes wait within two tiles of the doorway, never next to another of the player's heroes in the field and never on a tile that would cut a way through: one whose open neighbours fall apart into separate pieces without it (`_cuts_a_way`). Idle heroes left in the field used to fill the only exit of a dead end or a one-tile corridor and hold the main army there: in the owner's 2026-10-08 game Embercourt's lesser hero waited on the one tile linking its capital's pocket to the map and held the main army in for 34 days. A parked hero keeps its own small army; the town's troops stay in its garrison (computer towns do not use the human's garrison row).

### Fights

- Strength (`AiHeroRules.army_power`): per creature sqrt(health x average damage), scaled by the creature's attack and defense and 1.15 for ranged. The leading hero's attack raises the army's damage and its defense cuts the damage it takes, by the battle's own curve (`BattleDamageRules.attack_defense_multiplier`: 5% per attack point, 2.5% per defense point), and the strength takes the square root of both. Its damage spells add to the army's damage per round: the strongest battle damage spell it knows and can pay for, cast once a round over four rounds while its mana lasts (`_spell_damage_per_round`). Recalibrated 2026-10-08 after the battle stat scaling (uncapped attack and defense, stronger spells) on 1,600 auto-resolved battles of random hero-led armies of 10 to 1,000 strength: it calls the winner in 83% of them (86% when one side is small), against 74% for the old estimate (5% per point of the hero's attack plus defense, no spells). The old estimate let four creatures behind a caster look weak, so heroes kept attacking a human hero whose Cinder Burst then won every battle, and it overrated a might hero's attack and defense against guards. Won battles at a given strength ratio, from that sample: about 17% at half the defender's strength, 54% at even strength, 78% at 1.4x and 93% at twice.
- Neutral guards, dwelling guards, neutral town garrisons and rival AI heroes are fought with the human's auto-resolve: a disposable battle session (`BattleRules.create_battle_payload`, `BattleAutoResolveRules.resolve_active_battle`) with the AI hero on the attacking side. Survivors, experience, artifacts and rewards come from that battle. A lost battle removes the hero; a stalemate keeps both armies. Until 2026-10-09 auto-resolve checked the attacking hero's scored spell against `battle.player_hero`, the battle stat payload, which has no spellbook, so its mana read as the 8-point minimum: the 27 battle spells that cost more (the strongest damage spells, such as Bell Lance, among them) were never cast by the attacker, while a defending hero cast them. It now checks the commander state the cast spends from (`BattleRules._player_commander_state`); the human's Quick Resolve had the same fault.
- Every battle, the human's included, pays by the strength of the army beaten (`BattleRules.battle_rewards_for_strength`, owner decision 2026-10-08): half a point of experience per point of strength (the AI's army rating), so a typical generated guard of about 300 pays about what the encounter tables paid, and half of the encounter's own gold and resources scaled by the same strength against that typical guard (a quarter to four times). An enemy that withdraws pays for what fell. Beating an AI hero used to pay its template's flat 140 gold and 160 experience whatever its army.
- Attacks on human heroes and towns start a normal battle the human plays (`hero_intercept` / `town_defense`). A hero that wins a town assault captures the town and stays on the map as a hero.

### Towns and economy

- Each day: build a Tavern while the faction needs heroes, hire, recruit, then build. A hire appears at the town even when one of the faction's own heroes stands in the doorway: that hero is in the town, as with the human's visiting heroes. Recruits are bought at real cost from real availability into the garrison (highest tier first). A full garrison dismisses its weakest stack when the creatures it would buy are stronger (`_make_room`), as a human dismisses a stack from a full army. Until 2026-10-09 a garrison holding seven odd stacks (other factions' strays, two or three creatures left by heroes) bought nothing: in a four-player match (seed ai-match-3854663367) a captured town kept the same seven stacks for four weeks while 117 creatures piled up unbought, the two survivors banked 922,000 and 409,000 gold, and with neither army able to attack the other they traded five towns 82 times until the 160-day cap. With the fix the same match is decided on Day 77 after 24 captures. One build per town per day, through the player's build status check and cost: the affordable building with the best value for its price (two weeks of troops from a dwelling, upgrades, two weeks of income, spells, a market's trades, buildings it opens up). A town that musters little favors dwellings. Each market stage is worth what a Storehouse is (two weeks of its wood and ore), 1.8 times that while a missing resource holds up the build list, so the Marketplace (1,000 gold, `docs/town-market.md`) comes among a town's first builds and the Trade Exchange ranks with a Storehouse. A building counts each building of the town's tree it completes the requirements of; until 2026-10-08 only buildings already buildable counted, so a prerequisite such as the Marketplace scored nothing and no computer town ever built one. A dwelling upgrade is worth what the upgraded creatures add over the same two weeks, counted once: until 2026-10-17 the gain was added a second time, so Mireclaw, Thornwake and Veilmourn, whose upgrades add the most, upgraded their first dwelling in week 1 instead of opening a second, mustered one creature line in week 2 where the other factions mustered two, and got their tier-4 dwelling a week later.
- When a build or a recruit order is short, a town with a market trades for the difference with the human's quotes (`TownRules.market_trade_quote`, which covers every resource including rares): spare wood and ore convert first, then other spare resources, gold buys the rest, spare resources sell when gold is short, and the weekly usage and caps are recorded the same way as the human's orders.
- Income is the human's: owned towns, controlled sites and artifacts. On a generated map every computer player starts with the human's opening stockpile, 5,000 gold, 10 wood and 10 ore (`NativeRandomMapPackageSessionBridge._opening_enemy_states`; `ScenarioFactory` for the legacy generated path). Until 2026-10-08 they started with nothing. No pillage, no abstract siege capture, no pressure-driven spawns. Difficulty only changes what `DifficultyRules` defines for the enemy side (battle knobs).
- Enemy pressure is still read by scenario conditions, the command risk forecast and battle outcome adjustments, but nothing raises it any more: each computer turn resets it to 0.
- The town planner prices a building's cost and income at the market's valuation (`AiHeroRules._build_value`: gold at face value, wood and ore 400, rares 800), not the lower values heroes use for pickups. Priced at 80 a unit, wood and ore looked cheap, so a gold-rich computer player spent its ore on Town Hall levels and then sat on gold it could not build with (on the walled test map, 25,000 gold and 553 tiles explored by day 29; priced at market value, two Storehouses early, no hoard and 637 tiles).

## AI against AI

Balance batches use `tools/ai_match_batch.py --all-arrangements`: every faction permutation on each map (24 in 4P), complete faction-set cycles (360 games for one 4P cycle with six factions), and an explicit map-level roll seed. `HEROES_AI_MATCH_ROLL_SEED` is an evaluation-only flag: the native tavern and hire rules pin weekly hero priority, hire level, starting army and growth by map/faction/hero rather than slot or generated session identity. Availability and subsequent decisions remain gameplay-dependent; ordinary games and legacy plans keep their seeds. `--rotate --fixed-rolls` retains cyclic rotations only. Both the native runner and Godot preparation/direct paths record the applied seed. Reports bootstrap whole maps for score intervals; the old equal-faction bands are reference distributions, not confidence in a new map bank.


`tools/ai_match_batch.py --runner` uses a refilling pool of up to 16 native processes, one thread each. `--desktop` selects 8; `--slots N` explicitly sets the process limit. Native processes recycle after bounded lists (`--per-process`, default 32; Godot stays at 4), with each seed's rotations kept together even if a group exceeds a smaller limit. A list holds whole seed groups, heaviest first, up to an expected cost (map tiles x levels x players) of the work still queued over four lists per slot, so two-level extra large games come a few to a list and lists shrink near the end (2026-10-30; until then a list held up to a slot's share of the queued games, and in the 800-game batch of 2026-10-29 a few workers played 32 two-level extra large games each while the rest stood idle). The pool writes each result as it arrives, times out only the active native game, and requeues games a failed worker never began. `plan.json` saves the execution settings for resume; explicit flags override them. `inflight.json` tracks native workers and the content exporter as well as Godot preparation processes, so resume refuses while recorded processes remain alive. The content snapshot is shared read-only; map corpora and JSON weight sets keep their Godot preparation path, with setup batches limited to four games or a whole rotation group.

A match can be played with no human. `AiSeatRules.hand_human_seat_to_ai` gives the human's seat to the computer: the player record becomes a computer player, its towns become computer towns under the same player id, its treasury moves to its computer state and its heroes leave the map, so on its first turn it hires a start hero like every computer player. With no human seat, `HeroCommandRules.normalize_session` keeps no player heroes. Nothing ends such a match by itself; `AiSeatRules.standing_players` lists the players that still hold a town or a hero, and `match_over` is true once they are all on one team.

A session that sets `session.flags["ai_battle_log_enabled"]` keeps every auto-resolved fight in `flags["ai_battle_log"]` (both armies before and after, strength, the expected and actual loss, experience and resources) and every town capture in `flags["ai_capture_log"]`. Normal games set neither.

`tests/ai_versus_ai_match_report.gd` plays such a match on a small one-level generated map. The match seed draws both factions independently (a mirror match is possible; the generator never deals one faction twice, so the second seat's capital is set up as the drawn faction) and the map seed. Both seats start with the human's starting resources, as every computer player does. It writes the battle log, the town captures, hires and fallen heroes and a daily ledger per side (treasury, towns, buildings, heroes and their strength, garrison strength, sites, explored tiles) as JSON. `HEROES_AI_MATCH_SEED` sets the seed (`random` draws one) and plays to the end or `HEROES_AI_MATCH_MAX_DAYS` (default 320; 160 before 2026-10-16); `HEROES_AI_MATCH_OUT` sets the JSON path. `HEROES_AI_MATCH_SWAP=1` plays the same map with the two factions' starting places traded (both capitals are set up as the other faction's), to tell the map's balance from the factions'. Without a seed it draws one. `HEROES_AI_MATCH_PLAYERS` puts up to eight computer players on the map (default 2) and `HEROES_AI_MATCH_SIZE` picks the size class (default `homm3_small`); `HEROES_AI_MATCH_FACTIONS` (comma-separated faction ids, one per seat) replaces the seed's faction draw, so a batch can give every match a different line-up; `HEROES_AI_MATCH_GUARDS` sets the guard strength (`weak`, `normal`, `strong`; default `normal`, as a new game from the main menu; before 2026-10-09 the runner passed none and its maps had weak guards); `HEROES_AI_MATCH_WATER` (`land`, `normal_water`, `islands`; default `land`) and `HEROES_AI_MATCH_LEVELS` (1 or 2; default 1) pick the map's water and underground (until 2026-10-16 every match was land on one level); the record also holds a weekly snapshot of every town (owner, buildings, unbought creatures, garrison). The same seed replays the same match. `tools/ai_match_batch.py` plays batches of such matches (an explicit seed, setup and line-up per match in `plan.json`, many at once in throwaway profiles, a `summary.md` with each faction's finishing places and wins against a fair share); see the ai-match-batch skill.

First match, 2026-10-08 (seed `ai-match-4199114532`, Thornwake north, Veilmourn south): Veilmourn won on day 25 in 16 battles. Thornwake's capital had no troops in it when Veilmourn's main hero arrived on day 15; after that the two sides' couriers (5.5 strength each) took the empty town in turn, 14 times in 8 days, while Thornwake's main hero never went back for it. With the places swapped (`HEROES_AI_MATCH_SWAP=1`) Thornwake won on day 35. Under the owner's follow-up rules (computer players start with the human's resources, doubled market limits, town planner pricing at market value) the same pair went the same way, slower: Veilmourn on day 56, Thornwake on day 51. In all four games the southern main hero cleared the same four guards up the east side, (34, 26) to (29, 13), which ends at the northern capital; the northern main hero's guards lie mostly to the south-west. The northern capital fell first every time and then changed hands almost daily (57 of 62 captures unopposed in the last pair), so the map's layout decides who reaches the other capital first and the empty towns make it decisive.

## Save migration

- `EnemyTurnRules.normalize_enemy_states` runs `AiHeroRules.migrate_legacy_raids` once per save (`overworld.ai_heroes_version = 1`; new sessions are created with the marker). It is skipped while a battle is pending, because the battle refers to its armies as they are.
- A raid host with a commander becomes a hero: it keeps position, army, level and experience, gets the template's movement and a fresh goal, and drops every raid target field. A commanderless column joins a hero of its side within 3 tiles; troops that do not fit go to the side's nearest town garrison (or are lost if it owns none).
- Each computer turn also converts any host a scenario script placed later. Old pressure, raid counters and task boards stay in the enemy state but no longer drive anything.

## Player-facing text

"Raid" no longer appears in player-facing text: threat lines, briefings, town threat summaries, battle names and enemy activity summaries talk about enemy heroes and armies.

## Performance

Searches use flat byte masks per level: static blocking and site stops are built once per enemy turn and shared by all computer players; armies, guards and heroes are added per search. The search radius is three days of movement (seven when nothing is in reach). Fight previews are skipped at a 3x margin and the previewed battle is reused for the fight.

- `large_generated_map_runtime_profile_report`, one end turn: the rules part went from 1782 ms (raid model) to 903 ms; the whole confirm is dominated by the autosave (3991 ms before, 3779 ms after).
- Idle heroes run one more search when nothing is in reach (`_clear_way`), and a hero asked to step aside one for its move.
- `AI_PLAYER_RULES_REPORT` perf mode, `homm3_large`, three computer players, 21 days: the AI part of an end turn is 0.4 to 1.6 s on most days and 4.2 s at most (a day with five fight previews and five battles), against `END_TURN_LIMIT_MS` 15000. A preview costs 0.1 to 0.4 s with large armies, which is the main cost to watch.
- Guard valuation by what a fight opens and costs (2026-10-07), same perf run: median 1.2 s against 0.8 s before, worst day 3.0 s against 2.3 s. The computer players fight about twice as often (32 battles and 38 previews against 17 and 15); measuring the ground behind guards adds about a third to decision time outside previews.

It is not part of the full validation suite.
- Raid-model AI scenes are updated to hero behavior: live turn execution, known-world memory (scouting instead of exploration raids), assault grouping (column joins a hero, lesser hero hands troops to the main hero, also after walking), multi-scenario recruitment delivery (recruits reach hero armies via the garrison) and the strategic AI KPI generated-map probe (heroes fielded and objectives taken).
- `HeadlessSimulationHarnessRules` cases are all hero cases now: live turn, route progression, town build and recruit over three days, town retake assault, assault grouping and recruitment delivery, plus the rewritten raid cases: every enemy faction of each authored scenario fields a working hero (pressure coverage); a strong hero assaults the human town (objective targeting); an understrength hero reinforces from its garrison before fighting (regroup); the main hero holds the gate of a threatened town (town defense, single and multi-scenario); a lost site is retaken (resource-site defense). The fixtures pin the session id, because the AI tavern offers are seeded from it and it otherwise comes from the start time.
- The raid director's unit-level tests (task board, commander roles, raid regroup and defense retasks, adventure spells, empire economy runways, raid path plan reuse) were deleted with it. `tests/validate_repo.py` `validate_ai_hero_model` pins the hero-model owners and keeps the raid entry points out.

## Who stays in the game

On a generated map (`PlayerEliminationRules`, owner decisions 2026-10-09) a player is out when it holds no town and no hero, or when it has held no town for seven days in a row; the human sees a countdown each day and loses on the seventh, a computer player's heroes leave the map. Losing the starting hero does not end the game: the most experienced other hero leads, or the next one hired (`HeroCommandRules.hand_over_primary`); the fallen hero is not rebuilt from the scenario. Authored scenarios keep their own defeat rules. `tests/player_elimination_rules_report.gd` covers the five cases.

## Known gaps

- A hero with nothing it can win in reach and no frontier left idles at home; it does not plan a build-up to a target guard.
- A town that is not threatened is still left empty when the main hero takes its garrison (the owner's choice, again on 2026-10-28: troops travel with the heroes). Two main armies of about equal strength neither of which can attack the other (1.3x) can still trade an empty town back and forth.
- Garrisons of captured towns fill with what those towns muster, other factions' creatures the main army has no slot for, and no hero carries them out. After the 2026-10-20 Sunvault trim ai-match-4265064792 and ai-match-3080264325 run to the 320-day cap: in the first the two leaders hold 57,000 to 91,000 each in garrisons of seven full stacks against main armies mostly of 5,000 to 27,000, and 259 of the 485 captures after day 150 meet an empty town. Counting garrisons as strength for the hunt, or drawing the main army home for them, changed games from the first weeks and was dropped (2026-10-20, see A player far ahead hunts harder).
- Mines and other flaggable sites change hands back and forth between heroes the same way towns did; site defense is not planned.

- AI heroes do not sail into whirlpools (generated maps place none) and do not re-board within one planned route.
- Heroes do not plan around scenario objectives; they only value what is on the map. Three `validate_repo` pins for objective types (Great Work, Twin Command, relief routes) look for that in `AiHeroRules` and fail until it exists.
- Heroes still re-weigh their errands every day on one level: a threatened town and an own town's troops count for every hero however far it is, and two heroes can set out for the same garrison. Limiting a threatened town's defence to heroes within a day's march lengthened the eight replays from 1,709 to 2,180 days with two at the cap (2026-10-29), so it was left out: those draws seem to bring armies within reach of the raiders. With the gate commitment (see Levels) the same eight end on days 292, 320 (ai-match-23293556: the leader holds 26 towns from day 290 and does not find the last rival's five), 178, 259, 301, 165, 226 and 140, 1,881 days against 1,709; eight seeds say nothing about game length either way.
- Heroes do not cast adventure spells (movement or scouting), do not hunt the human's reinforcement convoys and do not send convoys of their own.
- All factions share one decision model. The per-faction strategy weights and priority targets in scenario enemy configs are not read.
- Artifacts are valued flat: set pieces and faction regalia are worth no more to the faction they belong to.
- A hero with a big one-shot damage spell beats a small army far stronger on paper: the strength estimate spreads spells over four rounds. Attacks on heroes discount the attacker by the share of its health one cast would take (`_burst_share`), and such fights are always previewed. On 2026-10-09 a hero with six cadets (88) attacked one with a single Shard Warden (18) unpreviewed and lost all six to one Bell Lance (77 base damage). The battle log now records both heroes' stats, spells and mana and how the battle ended.
- Skipping the fight preview at a 3x power margin can still lose a fight; a harness run saw a hero lose to the guard it did not preview. The loss estimate is calibrated for the average army; losses differ a lot by army (at a guard of 0.3-0.5 of the hero's strength Mireclaw's main hero lost 16% on average, Sunvault's 2%), and the preview corrects what the estimate misses.
- An island player can be shut in for good. Replayed on 2026-10-29 with a build of the reinforcement rules a step before their final one, ai-match-3008409200 (extra large 6-player, water) took another course and reached the 320-day cap (the final build ends it on day 178): the second player's heroes left their island in its one boat, the hero aboard fell elsewhere on day 150, and from then its heroes found nothing to do off the island (no boat, no shipyard in reach) with 200,000 gold in the treasury. Its main army grew from 22,000 to 74,000 in its town, and the last rival, holding five towns to its one, never reached the 1.4x needed to storm it.
- Couriers (lesser heroes that are not field armies) hand over what the receiver keeps and then wait at home to carry the next recruits, unless an own town holding troops or a main or field army lies within twelve days, on this level or past gates. Field heroes and the main hero take a town's garrison along when it is not threatened, so a town between armies is often empty: in the medium-map match one capital changed hands 13 times, mostly to small heroes walking into an empty town.
