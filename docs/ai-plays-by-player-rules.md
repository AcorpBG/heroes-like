# AI Plays By Player Rules

Owner feedback, 2026-10-07: enemy "raids" feel wrong; the AI should play like a human with real heroes that take mines, improve towns and fight map guards under the same rules as the player.

## Model before this change

- `EnemyTurnRules.run_enemy_turn` ran an "empire cycle" per computer player: income, weekly musters, town builds (with an AI-only pacing floor), a hero task board, raid reinforcement, abstract pressure gain, raid launches and an abstract siege counter.
- Enemy armies on the map were raid encounters (`session.overworld.encounters` entries with `spawned_by_faction_id`), spawned at towns once a pressure counter crossed a threshold. Their armies came from encounter templates such as "Mireclaw Raid", led by a commander from the faction roster.
- `EnemyAdventureRules.advance_raids` moved each raid one tile per day, chose targets through a large planner, "broke" neutral and dwelling guards by comparing strength numbers without losses, pillaged the player's treasury on arrival, and could capture towns through a siege counter.

## Model now (`scripts/core/AiHeroRules.gd`)

`EnemyTurnRules.run_enemy_turn` calls `AiHeroRules.run_faction_turn` for every computer player. The raid director is removed (2026-10-07). What is left in `EnemyAdventureRules` serves heroes and old saves: commander roster records, raid army normalization for the save migration, the site seizure `_secure_resource_target` and the AI event records. Its multi-level path surfaces are not used in play; the RMG transit and start placement validation tools plan journeys with them, and they are the starting point for heroes that cross levels. `EnemyTurnRules` keeps the turn loop, enemy state normalization and the threat summaries.

### Heroes

- An AI hero is an encounter entry (so map rendering, fog, battles against the human, saves and turn playback keep working) with an `ai_hero` block: hero id, movement `{current, max}`, home town, hire day, private goal and a short avoid list. Its hero record is `enemy_commander_state`; its army is `enemy_army`, made of real troops. The encounter template (`encounter_mire_raid`) only supplies battle settings; battles are called "Battle with <hero>".
- Every computer player starts with one hero and its faction's default starting army, like the human. More heroes are hired in a town with a Tavern through the shared hire path (`HeroCommandRules.tavern_offer` + `build_recruited_hero`), paying the template's gold cost, up to `HERO_LIMIT`. Wanted heroes: 1 + min(towns, 2), plus one from day 14.
- Movement uses the human's rules: `movement_max_for_hero` at normal difficulty, one point per tile, full budget each day. Pathing is an 8-way search with the overworld corner rule; water and rock are impassable, object bodies block, town doorways and site visit tiles can be stood on. Guard zones, armies and heroes end a route; the player's own heroes inside one of its towns (on the doorway) stand in no one's way. A site that holds nothing more for the hero (a picked-up cache, its own mine) can be walked across, and so can the doorway of a building that stands after a visit (shrines, schools, roadside producers, signs; `WALK_ACROSS_SITE_FAMILIES`): in the owner's 2026-10-08 game Embercourt's main hero entered a pocket whose only way out ran over a shrine's doorway and could not plan its way back.
- Knowledge is fog-honest. Each computer player keeps an explored map (`ai_explored`, base64 bitsets per level). Its towns see as far as the human's (`OverworldRules.TOWN_VISION_RADIUS`), its heroes their scouting radius along every step. Map objects count once explored; human heroes and rival AI heroes only while in sight. With nothing worth doing in reach, a hero walks to the nearest unexplored frontier.

### Choosing what to do

Each decision is a utility score: value / (1 + distance / daily movement), with a bonus for keeping yesterday's goal. Values are gold equivalents.

- Resource pickups, mines and other claimable sites (resources the faction is short of count 1.8x), dwellings (a week of troops) and visit buildings (what the building gives, in gold, only when the hero has the level and the faction the price it asks).
- Artifacts.
- Neutral guards and wandering armies the hero beats with a 1.3x power margin (1.5x before the strength estimate was recalibrated on 2026-10-08). A guard is worth its reward, plus what stands in its zone (sites, artifacts, a town doorway: on generated maps guards sit next to what they protect without a link), plus the ground it opens: open ground behind its zone the hero cannot reach today counts 300 and 4 for each unexplored tile, and sites already seen there count at half their value (up to 5000 in all). From that come the troops the fight should cost: Lanchester's square law (a defender r times the hero's strength costs 1 - sqrt(1 - r^2) of it) raised to what auto-resolve costs, 1.3 times the square law plus 1.2% (calibrated on 1,262 auto-resolved fights of the three computer players' main heroes against the guards of the owner's 2026-10-08 map: mean losses 8.7% at a guard of 0.3-0.4 of the hero's strength, 14% at 0.4-0.5, 22% at 0.5-0.6 and 32% at 0.6-0.7, about a third above the square law). A guard's reward is what the battle will pay for it (see Fights). Before setting out to fight a guard or a rival hero the hero runs the same auto-resolve on a scratch session (skipped at a 3x margin), and goes only when it wins, keeps at least half its strength and the fight still pays with the losses that battle really shows; otherwise it leaves that target for three days.
- Towns (power margin 1.4x over the garrison plus any hostile hero standing in the doorway), human heroes and other computer players' heroes in sight (1.3x). A town is worth what holding it gives (`_town_worth`): 4,000 plus two weeks of its income and of the troops its dwellings muster, priced as the town planner prices them, 2,000 more for the human's; a player that has lost every town values one twice as much. Until 2026-10-08 every town was worth a flat 4,000 (6,000 the human's), about what a mine is, so a hero that had lost its capital flagged mines for ten days with the empty capital ten tiles away. A rival hero is worth 1,200 plus half its army, plus half the worth of the most valuable of our towns it threatens. Town assaults and dwelling guards subtract the same loss estimate.
- Its own towns: the main hero collects the garrison, a lesser hero picks it up to carry it out when the main hero is more than a day's march away, and a hostile hero near the town makes it urgent. A visit is worth only what the hero can take along (`_garrison_pickup`): every stack that merges or fits, then the garrison's stronger stacks in place of the hero's weakest, as a human arranges seven slots. A full army used to walk back for garrison troops of other kinds it could not take, every day.
- Its own main hero: a lesser hero walks over and hands its troops to the main hero, keeping one creature of its weakest kind. A hand-over is worth at most what a modest site is worth (600), so lesser heroes finish their own errands first.

Lesser heroes act before the main hero each day, so troops they hand over are with the main hero when it acts. The most experienced hero among those holding at least half the strongest hero's army (the strongest army among equals) leads the main army. Going by army alone, a lesser hero carrying a big garrison out to the main hero became the main hero itself; going by experience alone, a veteran whose army had been beaten stayed "main" and an 821-strength hero handed its army to a town for it. A lesser hero near the main hero leaves all but its weakest stack in a town for it.

A computer player's hire gets its level-up growth seed from the session (`HeroCommandRules.new_growth_seed`, passed to `build_recruited_hero` before any levels it arrives with), as the human's hires do; without it each AI hero rolled a random seed and the same match seed played out differently (2026-10-08).

Town defense. A town is threatened when a hostile hero (the human's or another computer player's) that this player can see could reach its doorway next turn with more than 1.15x the garrison. In a threatened town no hero takes the garrison along and a hero standing in it defends it (an attacker fights that hero before the garrison). The main hero standing in it holds the gate while the threat is too strong to attack; a lesser hero would only be lost there and goes on. A weaker threat the hero can catch today it hunts; one it cannot catch today would walk back in behind it, so the hero leaves just enough creatures of one kind to beat it (at most a quarter of its own strength) and goes about its day, or stays when that would cost more. A hero that takes a town buys the town's available troops into the garrison at once, as a human does. Until 2026-10-08 computer players only saw the human's heroes as threats, and a town could be taken from under a hero standing in it, so in AI-against-AI matches 45 of 48 captures in 40 days met no defenders and a capital changed hands twice a day between couriers. With defense that match is decided on day 13 after one capture, past defenders; the Thornwake-Veilmourn pair goes from 37 and 25 captures to 11 and 3, and an 87-day Mireclaw-Brasshollow war with 69 captures (38 unopposed) to 37 days with 19 (12).

A guard's zone works as it does for the human: anything inside it is reached by fighting the guard first, and rock or an object's body inside the zone cannot be stood on (only a doorway or visit tile there). A fight that leaves the guard standing sends the hero back to the tile it attacked from. A hero hemmed in by zones fights the weakest of those guards when the odds are at least even: when it cannot move at all, or when its search reached every tile it can walk to and none of its towns is among them.

With nothing worth doing, a hero first takes a second look at up to two fights it dismissed on the estimate: the preview shows what each really costs, and one that pays after all, or that it wins keeping three quarters of its strength, is fought (standing still earns nothing).

A hero that still finds nothing searches again as if the player's own heroes were not on the map (`_clear_way`). When something worth doing lies past one of its idle heroes, that hero is asked to step aside, into its town or off the way: at once if it can still move today, otherwise first thing on its next turn (`ai_hero.make_way`).

Otherwise the hero walks back, over as many days as it takes, to its home town and parks inside it (`ai_hero.parked`): one idle hero per town, standing on the doorway, where the player's own heroes are in the town and in no one's way. Other idle heroes wait within two tiles of the doorway, never next to another of the player's heroes in the field and never on a tile that would cut a way through: one whose open neighbours fall apart into separate pieces without it (`_cuts_a_way`). Idle heroes left in the field used to fill the only exit of a dead end or a one-tile corridor and hold the main army there: in the owner's 2026-10-08 game Embercourt's lesser hero waited on the one tile linking its capital's pocket to the map and held the main army in for 34 days. A parked hero keeps its own small army; the town's troops stay in its garrison (computer towns do not use the human's garrison row).

### Fights

- Strength (`AiHeroRules.army_power`): per creature sqrt(health x average damage), scaled by the creature's attack and defense and 1.15 for ranged. The leading hero's attack raises the army's damage and its defense cuts the damage it takes, by the battle's own curve (`BattleDamageRules.attack_defense_multiplier`: 5% per attack point, 2.5% per defense point), and the strength takes the square root of both. Its damage spells add to the army's damage per round: the strongest battle damage spell it knows and can pay for, cast once a round over four rounds while its mana lasts (`_spell_damage_per_round`). Recalibrated 2026-10-08 after the battle stat scaling (uncapped attack and defense, stronger spells) on 1,600 auto-resolved battles of random hero-led armies of 10 to 1,000 strength: it calls the winner in 83% of them (86% when one side is small), against 74% for the old estimate (5% per point of the hero's attack plus defense, no spells). The old estimate let four creatures behind a caster look weak, so heroes kept attacking a human hero whose Cinder Burst then won every battle, and it overrated a might hero's attack and defense against guards. Won battles at a given strength ratio, from that sample: about 17% at half the defender's strength, 54% at even strength, 78% at 1.4x and 93% at twice.
- Neutral guards, dwelling guards, neutral town garrisons and rival AI heroes are fought with the human's auto-resolve: a disposable battle session (`BattleRules.create_battle_payload`, `BattleAutoResolveRules.resolve_active_battle`) with the AI hero on the attacking side. Survivors, experience, artifacts and rewards come from that battle. A lost battle removes the hero; a stalemate keeps both armies.
- Every battle, the human's included, pays by the strength of the army beaten (`BattleRules.battle_rewards_for_strength`, owner decision 2026-10-08): half a point of experience per point of strength (the AI's army rating), so a typical generated guard of about 300 pays about what the encounter tables paid, and half of the encounter's own gold and resources scaled by the same strength against that typical guard (a quarter to four times). An enemy that withdraws pays for what fell. Beating an AI hero used to pay its template's flat 140 gold and 160 experience whatever its army.
- Attacks on human heroes and towns start a normal battle the human plays (`hero_intercept` / `town_defense`). A hero that wins a town assault captures the town and stays on the map as a hero.

### Towns and economy

- Each day: build a Tavern while the faction needs heroes, hire, recruit, then build. A hire appears at the town even when one of the faction's own heroes stands in the doorway: that hero is in the town, as with the human's visiting heroes. Recruits are bought at real cost from real availability into the garrison (highest tier first). One build per town per day, through the player's build status check and cost: the affordable building with the best value for its price (two weeks of troops from a dwelling, upgrades, two weeks of income, spells, a market's trades, buildings it opens up). A town that musters little favors dwellings. Each market stage is worth what a Storehouse is (two weeks of its wood and ore), 1.8 times that while a missing resource holds up the build list, so the Marketplace (1,000 gold, `docs/town-market.md`) comes among a town's first builds and the Trade Exchange ranks with a Storehouse. A building counts each building of the town's tree it completes the requirements of; until 2026-10-08 only buildings already buildable counted, so a prerequisite such as the Marketplace scored nothing and no computer town ever built one.
- When a build or a recruit order is short, a town with a market trades for the difference with the human's quotes (`TownRules.market_trade_quote`, which covers every resource including rares): spare wood and ore convert first, then other spare resources, gold buys the rest, spare resources sell when gold is short, and the weekly usage and caps are recorded the same way as the human's orders.
- Income is the human's: owned towns, controlled sites and artifacts. On a generated map every computer player starts with the human's opening stockpile, 5,000 gold, 10 wood and 10 ore (`NativeRandomMapPackageSessionBridge._opening_enemy_states`; `ScenarioFactory` for the legacy generated path). Until 2026-10-08 they started with nothing. No pillage, no abstract siege capture, no pressure-driven spawns. Difficulty only changes what `DifficultyRules` defines for the enemy side (battle knobs).
- Enemy pressure is still read by scenario conditions, the command risk forecast and battle outcome adjustments, but nothing raises it any more: each computer turn resets it to 0.
- The town planner prices a building's cost and income at the market's valuation (`AiHeroRules._build_value`: gold at face value, wood and ore 400, rares 800), not the lower values heroes use for pickups. Priced at 80 a unit, wood and ore looked cheap, so a gold-rich computer player spent its ore on Town Hall levels and then sat on gold it could not build with (on the walled test map, 25,000 gold and 553 tiles explored by day 29; priced at market value, two Storehouses early, no hoard and 637 tiles).

## AI against AI

A match can be played with no human. `AiSeatRules.hand_human_seat_to_ai` gives the human's seat to the computer: the player record becomes a computer player, its towns become computer towns under the same player id, its treasury moves to its computer state and its heroes leave the map, so on its first turn it hires a start hero like every computer player. With no human seat, `HeroCommandRules.normalize_session` keeps no player heroes. Nothing ends such a match by itself; `AiSeatRules.standing_players` lists the players that still hold a town or a hero, and `match_over` is true once they are all on one team.

A session that sets `session.flags["ai_battle_log_enabled"]` keeps every auto-resolved fight in `flags["ai_battle_log"]` (both armies before and after, strength, the expected and actual loss, experience and resources) and every town capture in `flags["ai_capture_log"]`. Normal games set neither.

`tests/ai_versus_ai_match_report.gd` plays such a match on a small one-level generated map. The match seed draws both factions independently (a mirror match is possible; the generator never deals one faction twice, so the second seat's capital is set up as the drawn faction) and the map seed. Both seats start with the human's starting resources, as every computer player does. It writes the battle log, the town captures, hires and fallen heroes and a daily ledger per side (treasury, towns, buildings, heroes and their strength, garrison strength, sites, explored tiles) as JSON. `HEROES_AI_MATCH_SEED` sets the seed (`random` draws one) and plays to the end or `HEROES_AI_MATCH_MAX_DAYS` (default 160); `HEROES_AI_MATCH_OUT` sets the JSON path. `HEROES_AI_MATCH_SWAP=1` plays the same map with the two factions' starting places traded (both capitals are set up as the other faction's), to tell the map's balance from the factions'. Without a seed it runs 40 days of a fixed seed as a regression check. The same seed replays the same match.

First match, 2026-10-08 (seed `ai-match-4199114532`, Thornwake north, Veilmourn south): Veilmourn won on day 25 in 16 battles. Thornwake's capital had no troops in it when Veilmourn's main hero arrived on day 15; after that the two sides' couriers (5.5 strength each) took the empty town in turn, 14 times in 8 days, while Thornwake's main hero never went back for it. With the places swapped (`HEROES_AI_MATCH_SWAP=1`) Thornwake won on day 35. Under the owner's follow-up rules (computer players start with the human's resources, doubled market limits, town planner pricing at market value) the same pair went the same way, slower: Veilmourn on day 56, Thornwake on day 51. In all four games the southern main hero cleared the same four guards up the east side, (34, 26) to (29, 13), which ends at the northern capital; the northern main hero's guards lie mostly to the south-west. The northern capital fell first every time and then changed hands almost daily (57 of 62 captures unopposed in the last pair), so the map's layout decides who reaches the other capital first and the empty towns make it decisive.

## Save migration

- `EnemyTurnRules.normalize_enemy_states` runs `AiHeroRules.migrate_legacy_raids` once per save (`overworld.ai_heroes_version = 1`; new sessions are created with the marker). It is skipped while a battle is pending, because the battle refers to its armies as they are.
- A raid host with a commander becomes a hero: it keeps position, army, level and experience, gets the template's movement and a fresh goal, and drops every raid target field. A commanderless column joins a hero of its side within 3 tiles; troops that do not fit go to the side's nearest town garrison (or are lost if it owns none).
- Each computer turn also converts any host a scenario script placed later. Old pressure, raid counters and task boards stay in the enemy state but no longer drive anything.

## Player-facing text

"Raid" no longer appears in player-facing text: threat lines, briefings, town threat summaries, battle names and enemy activity summaries talk about enemy heroes and armies.

## Performance

Searches use flat byte masks per level: static blocking and site stops are built once per enemy turn and shared by all computer players; armies, guards and heroes are added per search. The search radius is three days of movement (seven when nothing is in reach). Fight previews are skipped at a 3x margin and the previewed battle is reused for the fight.

- `large_generated_map_runtime_profile_report`, one end turn: the rules part went from 1782 ms (raid model) to 903 ms; the whole confirm is dominated by the autosave (3991 ms before, 3779 ms after).
- Idle heroes run one more search when nothing is in reach (`_clear_way`), and a hero asked to step aside one for its move.
- `AI_PLAYER_RULES_REPORT` perf mode, `homm3_large`, three computer players, 21 days: the AI part of an end turn is 0.4 to 1.6 s on most days and 4.2 s at most (a day with five fight previews and five battles), against `END_TURN_LIMIT_MS` 15000. A preview costs 0.1 to 0.4 s with large armies, which is the main cost to watch.
- Guard valuation by what a fight opens and costs (2026-10-07), same perf run: median 1.2 s against 0.8 s before, worst day 3.0 s against 2.3 s. The computer players fight about twice as often (32 battles and 38 previews against 17 and 15); measuring the ground behind guards adds about a third to decision time outside previews.

A third case regenerates the owner's 2026-10-08 large map (seed 1041912569, the playthrough's session id) and plays the three computer players 24 days with the human idle: each builds a Marketplace (all on day 3), explores at least 1,000 tiles (about 1,480, 1,375 and 2,250 for Embercourt, Mireclaw and Sunvault; in the owner's game Embercourt never passed 443 and Mireclaw 1,118) and leaves no hero idle in the field for four days, and the tile that sealed Embercourt's capital pocket is recognised as one that cuts a way through. - Raid-model AI scenes are updated to hero behavior: live turn execution, known-world memory (scouting instead of exploration raids), assault grouping (column joins a hero, lesser hero hands troops to the main hero, also after walking), multi-scenario recruitment delivery (recruits reach hero armies via the garrison) and the strategic AI KPI generated-map probe (heroes fielded and objectives taken).

## Known gaps

- A town that is not threatened is still left empty when the main hero takes its garrison (the owner's choice: one strong army beats small garrisons). Two main armies of about equal strength neither of which can attack the other (1.3x) can still trade an empty town back and forth.
- Mines and other flaggable sites change hands back and forth between heroes the same way towns did; site defense is not planned.

- Heroes stay on their map level: native two-level transit is not used yet. AI heroes do not use boats, shipyards or whirlpools yet; water stays impassable for them even though the human can sail (`docs/water-travel.md`).
- Heroes do not plan around scenario objectives; they only value what is on the map. Three `validate_repo` pins for objective types (Great Work, Twin Command, relief routes) look for that in `AiHeroRules` and fail until it exists.
- Heroes do not cast adventure spells (movement or scouting), do not hunt the human's reinforcement convoys and do not send convoys of their own.
- All factions share one decision model. The per-faction strategy weights and priority targets in scenario enemy configs are not read.
- Artifacts are valued flat: set pieces and faction regalia are worth no more to the faction they belong to.
- Skipping the fight preview at a 3x power margin can still lose a fight; a harness run saw a hero lose to the guard it did not preview. The loss estimate is calibrated for the average army; losses differ a lot by army (at a guard of 0.3-0.5 of the hero's strength Mireclaw's main hero lost 16% on average, Sunvault's 2%), and the preview corrects what the estimate misses.
- Lesser heroes hand over all but one creature and then wait at home to carry the next recruits; they do not keep a fighting force of their own.
- Inert until the owner decides (keep, retarget or remove): Border Warden's `raid_pillage_resistance` and `HeroProgressionRules.scale_raid_pillage`, and the `DifficultyRules` enemy pressure and raid knobs (`enemy_pressure_bonus`, `raid_threshold_offset`, `raid_pillage_multiplier` and their adjust/scale functions). Two forecasts still read them, the public pressure gain (`OverworldRules._public_enemy_pressure_gain`) and the raid threshold in the command risk forecast (`EnemyTurnRules._raid_threshold_for_strategy`), and neither predicts anything real now.
