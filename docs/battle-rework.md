# Battle screen and battle flow rework

Slice: `battle-rework-design-20261009` (owner request, 2026-10-09: "We need to completely rework the BattleScreen and how battles play."). The owner signed off the mockup on 2026-10-09, accepted every recommended decision and asked for the right-hand hero to face the battlefield; the implementation slices below are being built in order.

Mockup (pass 2, signed off): https://claude.ai/artifact/Hy6UZnkYaLjXSwsAhqtsBF

## The owner's points

1. The battle shows in a small window, which breaks immersion.
2. Spells are cast by the units, wasting their turn.
3. The hero who actually casts the spells is not shown.
4. The battle order is shown only for the current turn; it should be updated all the time.
5. There is no Wait command.
6. A melee unit cannot choose the hex it ends on when it attacks.
7. Holding the right mouse button over a stack should show a preview with its details.
8. When a stack is selected and the pointer is over an enemy, the cursor should be a sword or an arrow (melee or ranged attack) with the estimated damage range in units.
9. Ranged stacks should show their shots left, and every stack its retaliations left.
10. The ground tiles need work, and blocking objects should exist.
11. It should be visible which enemy stacks are ranged and which are melee.

## Today's battle

Code facts at `9ad02be62`. BR = `scripts/core/BattleRules.gd`, BV = `scenes/battle/BattleBoardView.gd`, BS = `scenes/battle/BattleShell.gd`, TS = `scenes/battle/BattleShell.tscn`.

1. **Small window.** The board is the `BattleBoard` control (TS:277) in a framed panel between the banner (TS:53), a log row (BS:221) and a two-row footer with the action and system panels (TS:550). The hex grid is fitted inside the board at about 1.8:1, limited by its height (BV:5263-5269). At 1920x1080 the grid covers roughly 940x600 px, about a quarter of the screen, and units stand about 90 px tall; the rest is chrome and empty terrain. A Tactical Details sidebar (TS:345) is hidden by default.
2. **Spells end the stack's turn.** The commander already supplies the mana and spell power, and the once-per-round limit per side exists (`COMMANDER_SPELL_CAST_ROUNDS_KEY`, BR:72; `_commander_spell_cast_this_round`, BR:8658). But casting is offered only on one of the player's stack turns (`get_spell_actions`, BR:2187), the active stack plays the cast (`cast_player_spell`, BR:6055), and the cast ends that stack's turn (`return _complete_action(...)`, BR:6187, which calls `advance_turn`, BR:6688).
3. **No hero on the field.** BV has no hero or commander references. Hero data shows only in the hidden sidebar's command panel (BS:1937) and in the Spellbook dialog (BS:2409).
4. **Turn order for the current round only.** The board strip shows `upcoming_actor_ids(battle, 5)` (BR:2515, through `_turn_strip_entries`, BV:4632): at most five stacks still to act this round, and its comment rules out next-round ordering (BR:2513-2514). The order comes from `_sorted_turn_order` (BR:9412) in `_prepare_round` (BR:9369): initiative, then speed, then the player first; `_resort_remaining_turn_order` (BR:10305) re-sorts the rest of a round when initiative changes.
5. **No Wait.** Defend is worded as "the hold/wait order" (BR:5621). The orders are advance, strike, shoot, defend, retreat, surrender, move (which ends the turn) and cast (`perform_player_action`, BR:6231; `move_active_stack_to_hex`, BR:6189).
6. **The approach hex is automatic.** `melee_approach_destination` (BR:2052) takes the reachable cell next to the target with the fewest steps, ties going to the first found; `_apply_melee_approach` (BR:6382) applies it. The player only sees it as an END ring.
7. **No right-button details.** `_gui_input` (BV:303-324) handles pointer motion and the left button only. Hover tooltips show intent text, not stats (`_stack_board_tooltip`, BV:5702).
8. **No attack cursors; the forecast sits far from the pointer.** The only cursor is the global arrow (`_sync_pointer_cursor`, `scripts/ui/FrontierVisualKit.gd:269`). `attack_consequence_preview` (BR:2532) already returns the damage, losses and retaliation ranges and `spell_consequence_preview` (BR:2634) the spell outcome, but both are drawn in a caption box at the bottom of the board (BV:4739-4749).
9. **Shots and retaliations are hidden.** `shots_remaining`, `retaliations` and `retaliations_left` (BR:8555-8557) reset each round and show only in text summaries (`_stack_summary_line`, BR:15165) in the hidden rosters and the sidebar.
10. **Plain ground, no obstacles.** `_draw_terrain` (BV:2139) lays a terrain texture under the field plus hex tiles at 0.24 alpha; `_draw_hex_grid` (BV:2769) tints columns by distance band. Movement checks only bounds and occupancy; field objectives are markers that block nothing.
11. **Ranged and melee look alike.** A stack's plate shows only its name (BV:6011); the bow glyph appears only for units without art (BV:3048).

**D1 Full-screen battlefield.** The battle is one painted scene across the whole window. The board is scaled to a central field (about 74% of the width and 76% of the height); the heroes stand at the left and right edges, the ribbon runs along the top edge and the command bar sits at the bottom edge. No sidebars, caption plates or text panels over the field. Mockup: the whole screen; Board in the mockup pill compares 11x9 (proposed), 11x7 (today) and 15x11.

**D2 Heroes on the field.** Both commanding heroes stand beside their armies, full figure, each with a small plate: crest, name, mana bar, "Spell ready" or "Spell cast this round". Clicking the own hero or its plate, or pressing S, opens the spellbook; the hero glows while a cast is available and plays the cast, and a bolt flies from the hero to the target. Holding the right button on a hero or plate shows the hero card. An army without a hero (neutral guards) has no figure. Mockup: Lyra on the left, Vaska on the right.

Found while building the hero casting (2026-10-09): every encounter in content names an `enemy_commander` (204 of 204, 116 with starting spells), and a battle keeps it as the enemy hero, so guards have a commander who casts by the same rule as a hero. In a 111-battle benchmark on four generated small maps none of them knew a battle spell. Settled in the full-screen slice: only a hero from the content roster stands as a figure; a town captain or a guard's commander has none and shows only a plate, and only when it knows battle spells, so its casts still name it.

**D3 The hero casts and the stack keeps its turn.** One spell per round per side, cast by the hero during any of that side's stack turns, as a free action: the active stack then still moves, attacks, shoots, waits or defends. The cast leaves the stack's actions and becomes the hero's; creature abilities stay with the creatures. The enemy hero follows the same rule. Mockup: cast Cinder Burst, then attack with the same stack.

**D4 Live turn ribbon.** The ribbon always shows the active stack (large chip), the rest of this round in order, the waiting stacks, then "Round N+1" with the predicted order. It re-sorts at once when initiative changes (haste, Staggered) and when stacks die or wait, and the prediction includes effects running out. Chips carry the side colour, count, a bow or sword badge and a wait badge. Hovering a chip lights its stack and the other way round; holding the right button on a chip shows the stack card. Mockup: the top edge.

**D5 Wait.** Wait (W), once per round: the stack moves to the waiting segment and acts after every stack that has not waited, slowest first (HoMM3). A stack that waited cannot wait again that round. Defend (D) stays. Mockup: press W.

**D6 Directional melee approach.** With a melee stack active, where the pointer sits around an enemy picks the hex the attacker strikes from (six sides; it snaps to the nearest legal side). A ghost of the attacker stands on that hex, dots show the path, the sword cursor points at the struck side and the forecast says how far it walks; Q and E cycle through the legal sides. Two-hex attackers pick a head or tail placement. Rooted stacks strike only from where they stand. Mockup: point around an enemy with River Guard.

**D7 Right-button details.** Holding the right button on a stack, ribbon chip, hero or hero plate opens a card beside it; releasing closes it. Stack card: portrait, count, faction, tier, ranged or melee, large; top creature health and stack health; attack, defense, damage, health, speed and initiative with modifier deltas; shots and strike-backs left; cohesion and momentum; abilities; effects with turns left; waiting or defending. Hero card: portrait, class, mana, attack, defense, power, knowledge, specialty, spells. Mockup: hold the right button on any stack.

**D8 Cursors and forecast badge.** Sword (melee, turned toward the struck side), arrow (shot), broken arrow (penalised shot), move, spell, blocked, info. A small badge beside the cursor: "Kills 3-5" with "50-76 damage", then "Strikes back: lose 1-4 (12-41 damage)" or "No retaliation (reason)", then the steps; shots add "shots 8 -> 7"; spells show their damage and kills or their effect and turns. Blocked hexes say why. The first few hovers over a stack hint "Hold the right button for details". Mockup: hover enemies with a melee and a ranged stack.

**D9 Stack plates.** Every stack has a compact plate at its feet: side colour, a bow or sword icon, the count, shots left for ranged stacks, one pip per strike-back left this round (dim once spent), status icons, a wait or defend flag; the active stack's plate is lit. The bow or sword icon is also on ribbon chips, cards and the result screen. Mockup: every plate.

**D10 Painted ground and obstacles.** One painted battlefield per terrain, with a clear centre for the board and scenery at the edges where the heroes stand. The grid is a light etched overlay (Options: Full, Range only, Off). Obstacles are painted props on one or two hexes, chosen per battle from the terrain's set by the battle seed; they block movement and standing, never sit on deployment columns and always leave a path between the armies. Mockup: rocks and deadwood on the meadow.

**D11 Command bar.** Bottom centre, compact: Options, Surrender, Retreat, Auto | the latest log line (click: full log) | Spellbook with a mana badge, Wait, Defend. Hotkeys: Esc options, S or C spellbook, W wait, D or Space defend, A auto, R retreat, Q and E approach side, Ctrl to strike in melee with a shooter. Save, settings and the main menu move into Options. Mockup: the bottom edge.

**D12 Single-click orders.** Hovering already shows the whole outcome (cursor, ghost, path, forecast), so one click commits; no staged confirmation. Retreat and Surrender still ask.

**D13 Feedback.** Damage floaters ("-66", "5 killed") over the target, death animations, the log line and a short banner for rounds and casts. Battle speed Normal, Fast or Instant in Options.

**D14 Result.** A compact panel over the field: Victory, Defeat, Retreat or Surrender, one line of story, both sides' losses as unit icons with counts, then View the field and Continue.

**D15 Deployment.** Each army stands on its own edge: the first column (two-hex units on the first two), the enemy mirrored on the last; rows spread over the board height (11x9: rows 0, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8).

**D16 Retired.** The distance bands, the battle sidebars and caption plates, staged confirmations, and casting as a stack action.

## Rule changes

Rules stay in `BattleRules` and `BattleAiRules`; the screen only asks and shows. The AI plays by the same rules as the player.

1. **Board**: 11x9 (one constant pair), edge deployment and obstacle cells in the battle state, saved and loaded with it. Reachability, approach, placement and the AI treat obstacle cells like occupied ones. The distance bands go; the x0.6 shot penalty applies to a shooter next to an enemy instead of band 0. Obstacles are 2-4 rocks or deadwood of one or two hexes, seeded from the combat seed and the terrain with their own generator, so the damage rolls do not change. They stay off each army's two edge columns, never touch each other and always leave a two-hex body a way across. Town battles keep an open field. Advance walks toward the nearest enemy as far as the stack's movement allows. It stops next to the enemy for melee, two hexes away for reach, and out of contact for a shooter with shots. The old band survives only as a value derived from the stacks' positions, which field objectives still read; since the AI and balance slice the AI weighs each stack's own contact instead (`BattleAiRules._stack_contact_band`). It no longer sets up the battle or decides movement or shots.
2. **Wait**: a per-round waited flag and a waiting queue after the main order, slowest first; the round ends when both are empty. Waiting does not spend the stack's turn, so its effects do not count down. A turn-order forecast returns this round, the waiting queue and the predicted next round. The computer waits with a melee stack (or a shooter with no shots left) that cannot reach an enemy this turn while enemy stacks are still to act; since the AI and balance slice Auto battle and Quick Resolve wait the same way for the player's side.
3. **Hero casting** (built): the cast is the hero's free action. `cast_player_spell` is refused outside the player's stack turns and no longer advances the turn, so the active stack still gives its order; the once-per-round limit, the mana and the spell power stay as they are. The cast's events name the hero (`hero:player`, `hero:enemy`, `BattleRules.hero_actor_id`), so the log, the playback and the board show the hero as the caster and leave the active stack still; the casting hero's figure rises and flares and effects that travel start from it; a commander without a figure casts from half a hex past its army's edge. The enemy hero casts at most once per round, at the start of one of its stacks' turns, and that stack then gives its own order (`_run_enemy_turn`). The AI casts a spell that destroys its target, or one that scores at least the conservation bar (damage 2.0, control 2.25, recovery and cleansing 1.0, buffs 1.75, all 0.75 lower for a magic hero), and holds back a damage or control spell on a stack its own attack would destroy (`BattleAiRules._hero_cast_worthwhile`); the side Quick Resolve or auto-resolve plays casts a buff only while its stacks are under pressure (the AI and balance slice measured dropping that limit: in its 110-battle benchmark the side cast 229 spells instead of 108 and won the same 37 battles, so the limit stays). Buffs are timed to the turn: a buff on the acting stack scores a point more, since the effect does not spend the turn it lands in, and haste 2.5 more when its speed lets the acting stack reach an enemy this turn (`BattleRules.extra_speed_reaches_strike`). Quick Resolve and the computer players' auto-resolve cast and then give the same stack its order.
4. **Effect turns**: today's rule that an effect landing on the active stack does not spend its current turn (`docs/battle-spell-effects.md`) now also covers hero casts, which no longer end that turn: a haste cast before moving speeds up this move and the next turn. Players read it as "until the end of its next turn".
5. **Melee orders** (built): `melee_approach_cells` lists every hex a stack can strike a target from this turn: where it stands when it can already strike from there (two hexes off counts with reach or a hookline), then each hex next to the target it can reach, in walking order. A stack that walks always ends next to the target, so reach strikes from two hexes off only from where the stack stands, as before. A two-hex body lists each placement by its head's hex and says whether its head or its tail strikes (`strike_part`); a rooted stack strikes only from where it stands. A strike order can name its hex (`perform_player_action(session, "strike", approach)`, or the destination of `perform_presented_action`); a hex not on the list is refused and changes nothing. Without one, today's nearest-cell choice applies (`melee_approach_destination`); since the cursor slice the screen sends the hex the pointer picked. The computer picks among the listed hexes by the expected trade (`BattleAiRules.choose_melee_approach`): the units it destroys times their damage, minus the strike-back's, plus a quarter of the volley of each enemy shooter it ends up next to (whose shots then take the x0.6 penalty); ties go to the shorter walk. The enemy's strike and advance orders and the strikes of Quick Resolve and auto-resolve use that choice, so a stack with reach facing a target without reach strikes from where it stands instead of stepping in, and a stack steps around its target to stand next to a shooter. In a 110-battle benchmark on four generated small maps, the side Quick Resolve plays won 35 battles instead of 38: the guards' melee stacks gain the most. Since the AI and balance slice an advance by either side strikes the best target in reach (`BattleRules.ai_advance_plan`), so Quick Resolve's advance walks and strikes as the enemy's does.
6. **Previews** (built): `attack_consequence_preview` and `spell_consequence_preview` grow into one hover forecast: damage and kill range, strike-back range or why there is none, shots after, path and steps for the chosen approach, or why the order is illegal. They never draw from the battle RNG. The attack preview takes the approach hex and returns it with the steps, the path, `strike_part`, the shots before and after, and whether a shot is point-blank (`penalised`); both previews return `forecast_lines` for the badge, and `forecast_text` joins them into plain text.

**Cursors and forecast** (built, slice 6). The battle view draws the order under the pointer as its own cursor, hides the system cursor over the field and shows the forecast badge beside the cursor. One click gives the order the badge describes.
- Cursors: sword (melee), arrow (shot), broken arrow (point-blank shot), boot (move), spark (spell target) and blocked, which says why ("Blocked by an obstacle", "Out of reach this turn", "Rooted: cannot move", "No shots left, and out of reach this turn"). A stack no order applies to keeps the system pointer and names the stack and its role ("Your stack · Melee"); the player's hero shows the pointing hand and the spellbook hint; scenery shows the plain pointer.
- Melee approach: where the pointer sits on the target picks the side it is struck from, the legal hex whose direction from the target's hex under the pointer is nearest the pointer's (with the field's squash taken out), so a blocked or occupied side snaps to the nearest legal one. Within a quarter of the hex's radius from its middle the nearest hex holds: where the stack stands, else the fewest steps. A faded ghost of the attacker stands on the chosen hex, dots mark the walk and a ring its end, and the sword points along the blow. Q and E turn the side clockwise and back; the turn holds while the pointer stays in the sector it was made in. With the keyboard cursor on an enemy, Q and E turn the side and Enter strikes. The order carries the hex, which the rules check against `melee_approach_cells`.
- Badge: "Kills 5–10" with "52–103 damage" (or "Destroys the stack", or "· may destroy"); "Strikes back: lose 2–8 (23–89 damage)", with "if any survive" when the strike may destroy the target, or "No retaliation" with the reason (it is destroyed, it has struck back this round, Staggered, out of its reach), and for a shot "No retaliation · shots 4 → 3"; then "Moves 3 hexes first" or "Strikes from where it stands" (with "its tail strikes" for a two-hex body), and "point at another side, or turn it with Q and E" when there is a choice. A point-blank shot adds "Point-blank: -40% damage · hold Ctrl to strike in melee". A spell shows its name and mana, its damage and kills or its effect, and that the stack keeps its turn. A move shows no badge, only the walk.
- Ctrl: a shooter next to an enemy shoots at x0.6 by default; while Ctrl is held the cursor turns into the sword, and a Ctrl-click strikes in melee from where the shooter stands and keeps its shots.
- Spells: after a spell is chosen in the spellbook its targets light up; over a target the spark and the spell's forecast show, over another stack "Not a target for this spell", over empty ground "Choose a highlighted stack". A click casts and the stack keeps its turn.
- Removed: the line from the active stack to the selected target, which stayed on while the pointer was elsewhere.

- Plates: in the side's colour, the bow or sword, the count, a ranged stack's shots left after an arrow (dimmed at none), a diamond per strike-back a round (gold while the stack still has it, empty once spent or while Staggered), an hourglass while it waits, a shield while it defends, then up to two effect icons on their polarity foundation (a status's badge art, a spell's icon) and "+N" for the rest. The acting stack's plate glows gold; the plate of the stack pointed at, its ribbon chip, the keyboard cursor or an open card is ringed. `BattleGlyphs` draws the bow, sword, hourglass, shield and arrow for the plates and the cards.
- Cards: the right button held on a stack, its ribbon chip, a hero or a hero's plate shows a card until it is let go anywhere, the window loses focus or the result shows; the drawn cursor and the forecast step aside meanwhile. A stack's card shows its medallion, name, faction, tier, role (and Large), the count, the top creature's and the stack's health; attack, defense, damage, health, speed, initiative, shots and strike-backs, each with its change from the unit's own value; cohesion and momentum; abilities; each effect with what it does and how long it has left ("1 turn after this one" on the acting stack, "until the round ends", "3 rounds", "for the battle"), Defending and Waiting; and the hero's bonus it includes. The numbers are the battle's (`BattleRules.stack_details`): attack and defense as a blow counts them, with the hero's bonus and the Defend stance, and speed as the hexes the stack may walk this turn. A hero's card (`BattleRules.hero_details`) shows the portrait, name, faction and class, mana; attack, defense, power and knowledge; specialties with their ranks; battle spells with icon and cost, dimmed when the mana is short; and whether it has cast this round.
- Place: beside a stack (right, else left), under a chip, beside a hero or its plate toward the field, always between the ribbon and the command bar. The card keeps up with the battle while it is held, so an enemy turn's changes show at once. While a spell waits for its target the right button puts the spell away instead.
- Hint: over a chip, a hero or a stack no order applies to, the forecast ends with "Hold the right button for details" for the first six such hovers of a battle, and always over the enemy's hero.
- Choices: a Staggered stack's strike-backs show as spent (0/1); speed's base is the unit's speed up to 6; an effect that outlasts the battle reads "for the battle". The hero plate says "Spell cast this round", as the hero's forecast does.
- Validation: `tests/battle_plates_details_report.tscn` on a generated map. The rules' own orders set up the round (the river guard defends, the pikewards wait, the hero casts Stone Veil on the acting archers), with a Rooted, Staggered and Harried brute, a two-hex stack that has struck back and a roster hero leading the enemy. It checks every plate against the rules' state and every card's text (stacks, a chip, both heroes, both plates) against values worked out from the rules' numbers, then the card's place, the ringed plate, the plain pointer while the card is open, closing on release anywhere, the live update while the archers defend and the enemy plays, the right button putting a spell away, and the hint's count. It captures at 1920x1080 and 1280x720 and passes on Windows and on Linux (Vulkan on llvmpipe under xvfb), with the other battle reports. `tests/validate_repo.py` now expects the status badges on the plate and the card closed at the start.

**Battlefields, props and the hex grid** (built, slice 8). Every battle terrain is fought on one of eleven painted battlefields, each with its own props.
- Battlefields: the meadow (grass and plains; the painted meadow of the signed-off mockup), forest, swamp (swamp and marsh), mire, rough (rough, hills, highland and badlands), road, snow, coast, sand (now a desert of its own), lava and underground. An unknown terrain gets the meadow. Each painting was generated with the built-in image tool from one prompt template, taking its camera, layout and rendering from the meadow and its palette from the family's old ground texture. `art/battle/source/generated/battlefields_20261010/production.json` records each prompt, its references, the generation original and the master's sha256; the masters stay out of Git. `tools/build_battlefield_art.py` crops each master to 16:9 and resizes it to 1920x1080. The board covers the window with it, cropped to the window's shape and never stretched, under the vignette; without a painting the hexes get the terrain's colour.
- Props: each battlefield has three one-hex and two two-hex props of each kind (rock, deadwood), generated as one transparent sheet per kind with the battlefield as reference. The tool cuts each prop out by its alpha, scales it so that its ground tuft spans its hexes (216 px per hex width) and puts the tuft's centre on a fixed canvas anchor. The board draws that anchor on the hex centre, or on the seam of a two-hex obstacle, with a soft shadow under it; the obstacle's variant picks the prop (variant mod 3, or mod 2 for two hexes). `recipes.json` records each crop, scale and runtime sha256. The obstacles' rules are unchanged.
- Hex grid: the Options hold Full (every hex), Range only (the hexes the player's acting stack can move to, while no spell is aimed) and Off (no grid and no movement range). It is saved with the settings (schema 15, `gameplay/battle_hex_grid`, Full by default).
- Ambience: the desert has its own dust motes; every battlefield uses its family's motes.
- Package: lossy WebP imports (battlefields 0.82, props 0.85) without mipmaps, with the import files tracked. The export gains 7.8 MB (4.0 MB battlefields, 3.8 MB props) and drops the 14.8 MB tiled terrain textures, which stay in `art/battle/terrain` as source but are excluded from both release presets. Git gains 51 MB of runtime PNGs.
- Retired: the tiled terrain textures on the board, the overworld blocker components the obstacles used since slice 1, and the board's terrain-context and backdrop validation summaries. `tests/validate_repo.py` checks the battlefield model, every terrain's family, the production record, the recipes against the runtime art and its import settings, the grid modes and the Options wiring instead.
- Validation: `tests/battle_battlefield_art_report.tscn` on a generated map checks every terrain's battlefield, every painting and prop (size, and the anchor row through the ground tuft), each obstacle's drawing against its hex centre or seam and the painting's crop at 1920x1080, 1280x720, 2560x1080 and 1280x1024, the three grid modes (all 99 hexes; the legal hexes, none while a spell is aimed; none and no movement range), and the Options buttons with the saved setting. It captures each battlefield with the obstacles the rules make on it and with all ten of its props, the grid modes and the Options, at 1920x1080 and 1280x720. It passes on Windows and on Linux (Vulkan on llvmpipe under xvfb), with the other battle reports.
- Left for later: the hex grid is set only in the battle's Options, not in the main menu's Settings.

**AI and balance** (built, slice 9). Both sides' AI plays the new flow: Auto battle, Quick Resolve and the computer players' auto-resolved battles pick the player's side's orders with the same choice as the enemy's turns. No unit or rule numbers change.
- One order choice: `BattleAiRules._stack_order_choice` picks a stack's order for the enemy's turns and for the side Quick Resolve plays: its best attack, an advance or a defend. The best attack is a shot, a strike from where the stack stands or, for a stack that fights hand to hand, a strike after walking to the hex `ai_melee_approach` picks, scored as given from that hex (`_strike_candidate`). An engaged stack attacks, and so does one pressing a decisive attack, now on both sides. A stack that fights hand to hand and can reach an enemy strikes rather than advances, so an advance always means that no enemy is in reach.
- Advance: `BattleRules.ai_advance_plan` carries an advance out for both sides: the best strike in reach (`BattleAiRules.best_reachable_strike`; the expected trade puts an exposed shooter before a nearer melee stack), else Wait while enemy stacks are still to act this round, else a walk. The walk heads for the enemy with the fewest walking steps and ends on the reachable hex with the fewest steps left to it, so stacks go around obstacles instead of into them (`_walk_steps_to_target`, a search over the hexes the stack's body fits on). A shooter with no shots left counts as a melee stack throughout.
- Own contact: the AI scores a stack by its own distance to the nearest enemy (`_stack_contact_band`: next to one, up to five hexes, farther), not by the closest pair anywhere on the field. A stack across the field no longer braces as if engaged, and a shot counts as point-blank only from next to an enemy. Field objectives and the AI's copies of their rules still read the battle's band.
- Battle clock: in the last three rounds before stalemate a defend scores 2.5 less and a melee advance 1 more for each round closer (`_clock_pressure`), so a side that can still win closes in instead of letting the clock run out.
- Hero casts: a buff on the acting stack and a haste that brings it into reach score more, and the player's side keeps its pressure limit on buffs (rule 3).
- Quick Resolve's loop guard (`BattleAutoResolveRules.DEFAULT_STEP_LIMIT`) is 240 steps instead of 72: up to seven stacks a side may wait and then act in each of twelve rounds. The battle clock ends every battle first.
- Balance: the shooters' edge in auto-resolved battles came from melee stacks walking into their volleys one at a time. Melee stacks now wait for contact and then rush the best target in reach, shooters first, so the shooters keep their numbers.
- Benchmark: 110 battles by Quick Resolve, Caelen's starting army against every guard of four generated small maps. Victories 35 to 37, hero defeats 71 to 73, stalemates 4 to 0, 3.20 to 2.84 rounds a battle. The side lost 47.9% of its health (47.4% before) and the guards 45.2% (42.6%); in its victories it lost 27.0% instead of 30.5%. 105 outcomes stay the same: a stalemate and a defeat become victories, and three stalemates become defeats as the guards press before the clock runs out. Orders given: 249 shots, 135 strikes, 104 waits, 102 advances and 27 defends.
- AI against AI: 16 matches of `tests/ai_versus_ai_match_report.tscn` (seeds `s9-match-1` to `s9-match-16`, small maps, normal guards) before and after. All 16 were decided both times. The computer players lost 8 of 439 fights instead of 12 of 397, and 13.1% of their strength per fight instead of 15.6%. Matches ended on day 34.4 instead of 30.6 on average, and 4 of the 16 changed winner as early fights went differently. Each match plays out identically on Windows and Linux. Against guards they lost 14.2% of their strength where the guard valuation (`docs/ai-plays-by-player-rules.md`, calibrated before this slice) expected 19.6% (15.2% against 19.1% before); their decision to fight rests on a previewed battle, so the valuation stays as it is.
- Cost: the AI now scores a strike from each approach hex and walks around obstacles, so computer turns take longer. Each order reads the reachable hexes once (`_stack_order_choice` and `best_reachable_strike` hold a board read scope), and an advance's walking search shares one map of the hexes the stack fits on (`BattleRules._walk_fit_cells`) and reuses the chosen target's walk. Every battle plays out the same with and without these. Quick Resolve takes 141 ms a battle on the benchmark (101 ms before the slice, 150 ms without the shared reads). On Linux, on a large generated map over 21 days, the computer players' turns take 1.59 s a day instead of 1.32 s (slowest day 3.3 s instead of 2.3 s), a previewed battle 256 ms instead of 185 ms, and End Turn 2.2 s instead of 1.9 s on average and at most 4.0 s instead of 2.8 s. The longest computer turn in the 16 matches is 4.4 s instead of 2.6 s, against the 15 s end-turn limit. Most of what remains is the approach scoring's damage estimates and reachable-hex reads (`_estimate_damage`, `_reachable_empty_hexes`), which this slice did not change.
- Validation: `tests/battle_ai_flow_report.tscn` (44 checks) on a generated map: the best target in reach (a shooter before a nearer melee stack) for both sides; Wait, then a three-hex walk once no enemy is left to act; a shooter with no shots strikes or waits; a route around a rock wall, for both sides; own contact; the clock; buff timing with Quickmarch Hymn; Auto battle in the battle screen striking the shooter from the plan's hex; and Quick Resolve finishing a generated battle with no invalid order. `battle_melee_approach_report` now expects the enemy's advance to strike the player's shooter, and `battle_wait_turn_order_report` checks reach with `best_reachable_strike`. The battle suite matches the previous results on Windows and Linux, apart from one more Auto battle order in `battle_screen_report` and a buff valued decisive instead of strong in `magic_ai_valuation_casting_hooks_report` (`battle_event_animation_state_report` and `battle_intent_forecast_report` fail with the same errors as before).
- Match driver: `tests/generated_full_match_quality.py` stalled `quality_medium` from day 24 and stopped it on day 38 because it never answered the hero level-up dialog, which since 2026-09-20 opens over the field after a battle and holds every field order. (The cursor-forecast slice's notes blamed a guard approached from the hero's own square; that was wrong.) The driver now answers it as a player does, with the first specialty offered or Continue, and writes its `res://` paths with forward slashes, so it also runs on Windows. With the fix, `quality_medium` plays to its end on both platforms: the computer's raid (`encounter_mire_raid`) defeats the player on day 31 on Windows and day 32 on Linux, against day 52 and day 61 with the previous rules. The two rule sets part at the day-5 guard fight, and the raid first attacks on day 24 instead of day 35. Windows and Linux part from each other (on day 28 with the slice's rules) because the starting hero's growth seed is random outside a session (`HeroCommandRules.new_growth_seed`), so its level-up gains differ from run to run. Every run fails only the runtime error gate, on an overworld shader error that predates the slice (`OverworldCreatureIdle.gd:48`). Fixed 2026-10-10: the creature idle shader passed the built-in `TEXTURE` into a helper function, which the headless renderer's shader compiler cannot map to a sampler; it now samples `TEXTURE` in `fragment()` with the same math, and `quality_medium` passes every acceptance check on Windows.
- Left for later: the owner's playtest, and faction balance through the real battle rules (`tests/battle_faction_fast_balance_benchmark.py` models distance bands, not movement, obstacles or Wait).

The sword cursor turns toward the struck side and a system cursor cannot rotate, so the battle view draws its own cursor and badge over the field and hides the system cursor there. The rework is GDScript and art only, with no native code, so Windows and Linux behave the same.
