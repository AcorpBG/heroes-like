# Fluid creature animation

Owner request, 2026-09-23: expand the animation improvement to every live unit that falls short, including base creatures, town upgrades and neutrals. This supersedes the original 72-upgrade-only scope of the active goal. The whole live roster currently contains 232 creatures.

## Completion standard

- Idle: 8-12 distinct coherent poses with visible articulated breathing, arms/equipment or anatomy-appropriate movement. Eight existing poses alone do not establish visual acceptance.
- Movement: at least eight poses with a readable gait, flight, crawl or other suitable locomotion, stable ground/body reference and no teleporting limbs. Cut a walk clip to one clean gait cycle. The board sets the travel pace, not the clip: every unit crosses a hex in 600 ms at Normal speed (`STACK_MOVE_HEX_MSEC`), and the walk clip loops underneath at 1.25 times its authored timing (owner report, 2026-10-10: clips cut from 0.5 to 5.2 seconds of video had made units cross a hex in 0.5 to 5.2 seconds).
- Attack and relevant ranged/cast/support actions: 8-12 poses with anticipation, contact/release and recovery, preserving equipment and matching the actual attack type. A noncaster's support cue must remain a physical gesture rather than invented spellcasting. A melee-only unit does not require an invented ranged weapon.
- Hit: 4-6 distinct recoil/recovery poses. Defense: at least four dedicated guard/brace transition poses.
- Death: 8-12 poses forming a continuous collapse, ending in a grounded persistent corpse. The corpse may reuse the last death frame; death must not reuse the hit clip as its entire sequence.
- Video frame counts may exceed the phase targets above when needed for fluid motion; keep enough observed frames within texture/memory limits. Phase counts describe meaningful action coverage, not a hard cap on sampled frames.
- No duplicate-frame padding, ping-pong reuse counted as new artwork, whole-sprite bobbing/warping, crossfades alone, or idle aliases counted as completed movement/attack/cast/defense. Preserve qualifying existing sequences after inspection.
- Original paintings must retain creature identity, camera, equipment, anatomy, body size and surface style across frames. Preserve original sources, prompts and reference lineage. Small cropping/packing/alpha preparation is production tooling, not replacement artwork.
- Playback must give the new poses time to appear and synchronize attack contact, reactions, travel and death. Normal/Fast/reduced-motion modes must remain readable. Animation stays presentation-only and must not change simulation RNG, occupancy or saved gameplay.

## Production and ownership

The agent owns routine visual review, defect correction and publication. Do not ask the owner to approve each movement or action clip. Inspect the complete sequence, enlarged details, loop boundaries and native-scale renders, then publish accepted work autonomously. If a review route is unavailable, state exactly what was inspected and what remains unverified; do not present sampled frames as continuous playback.

`art/units/source/generated/fluid_animation/production.json` contains the current `assignments` partition for all 232 IDs. The initial pass used nineteen artists with 12-13 disjoint units each and one playback/pipeline agent. The owner subsequently stopped all subagents because of usage consumption; continue solo unless explicitly reauthorized. These assignments supersede the original four 58-unit batches; source paths retain their original batch names for provenance. Artists own only their assigned unit directories and handoffs. The coordinator owns shared data merges, final acceptance, planning, wiki updates and Git. Use existing units/manifest references rather than inventing new creature designs.

The shared hero-spell route presents the active stack in `cast_support_anchor` even for noncasters. Every creature therefore needs an appropriate physical support gesture; only actual casters should depict casting, and only ranged units require a ranged weapon/release sequence.

Owner direction after the H3 comparison: use local MiniMax H3 video in ComfyUI as the main production workflow for all creature action types. Generate action-specific video from original identity/key-pose guides, extract observed frames, remove the background and review before integration. Use the built-in image tool for missing reference poses and targeted repairs. Preserve lossless originals, exact workflow/model/seed/prompt, guide hashes, frame timestamps and matte recipes. Review temporal playback at real battle and map sizes. The initial Heartseed candidates remain rejected for equipment drift; choosing H3 as the default does not approve them. See `docs/video-creature-animation-trial.md` and the `heroes-creature-animation` skill.

Store each unit's sources under its assigned batch directory. A durable `handoff.json` describes the unit ID, source image paths, exact frame rectangles, anatomical ground anchors, clip names and ordering, shared scale/reference size and timing recommendations. Runtime atlas preparation must preserve normal inherited Windows file permissions and bound texture dimensions; Linux paths and metadata must stay portable.

Optional `pose_anchor_x` records the anatomical horizontal origin when a dense atlas cannot fit with symmetric transparent padding. The packer trims only empty margins before requiring another page; it never removes observed frames or shrinks anatomy to fit. Battle drawing reflects the anchor with enemy sprites, persistent corpses use the same origin, and overworld idle extraction subtracts its shared crop from that origin. Existing rows without the field retain a centered origin.

When a common grid still exceeds 4096px, pack original ink rectangles with four-pixel transparent gutters. `pose_frame_rects` maps the unchanged clip indices to storage rectangles, `pose_region_anchors` maps each rectangle's `x,y` to its original anatomical anchor, and `pose_sheet_size` records the actual texture dimensions. The common frame size remains the presentation envelope; storage trimming must not resize, rotate, normalize or discard any frame. Legacy grid rows remain supported unchanged. Battle, mirrored corpses and shared-crop overworld extraction use the same source offsets on Windows and Linux. If the actual ink cannot fit, require split pages rather than shrinking art.

A unit's idle may play from its own sheet instead of the atlas. These are the RIFE idle loops, owner direction 2026-10-11; see `docs/idle-inbetweens.md`. Such a clip carries `sheet`, `columns`, `frame_size`, `ground_margin` and `anchor_x`, plus `from_atlas`, the atlas idle the loop was made from. Battle and the overworld both draw that sheet, while the other clips keep the atlas. A republish keeps the loop unless it delivers a new idle, which drops the loop and deletes its sheet. After a new painted idle, run `tools/idle_inbetweens.py` and `tools/integrate_idle_inbetweens.py` for that unit; an H3 idle needs no loop.

Clips filmed on a blue plate had purple-pink edges on red and orange parts (owner report, 2026-10-11): the produce.py matte measured backdrop as blue minus max(red, green), so blue mixed into a red edge counted as figure. `tools/rematte_blue_plate.py` rebuilds those frames from the archived lossless originals. It first reruns the unit's own `process()` and requires the old matte to match the archive exactly. Then it re-solves only the edge band: an edge pixel takes the colour of the nearest clean opaque pixel, and only its opacity is solved; inside the figure nothing changes. A take whose plate is cyan rather than blue (its blue barely exceeds its green) never had the fringe and is left as it is. The frames go to `<take>/matte_blue_edges/`, with the recipe and hashes in `<take>/matte_blue_edges.json`. With `--publish` the tool republishes only the changed clips with the same frames, timing, anchors and scale, then registers them again. The frames are archived in the 2026-10-11 source snapshot (see `docs/source-material-storage-map.md`), not kept in the checkout. Restore them with `tools/restore_archived_source_material.py --prefix <take>/matte_blue_edges/`, or rebuild them with the tool; the recorded SHA-256 confirms either. New takes on a blue plate should use the same edge rule.

`tools/h3_creature_animation.py` is the shared H3 tool (2026-10-11). It replaces the per-unit produce.py, stage_video.py and prepare_handoff.py copies. A unit is described by one `<unit_dir>/h3_unit.json`: the identity and plate prompts, canvas, scale, seed, and one entry per take with its clip, its painted reference frames (names from the live reviewed handoff), its guide frames and its action prompt. Takes are only ever appended: a take's seed is the unit seed plus its position in the list. `prepare` renders the guides from the accepted paintings at their accepted anchor and scale, fitting one anchor for the whole unit (`h3_layout.json`). `generate` queues every sampling job at once (`--batch` adds other prepared units, so several units can render unattended), frees the models once, then decodes, keeps the lossless original, mattes with the blue-plate unmix plus the edge rule above, and draws review sheets under `.artifacts/h3/<unit>/`. `sheets --frames 28-40` draws a close-up of a few frames. The chosen frames go in `h3_selection.json`, and `build` writes the candidate `handoff.json` from it; publish as usual. Every stage records its seconds in `<take>/timing.json`, and `profile` prints them. An H3 idle needs no RIFE in-betweens: use every second video frame at about 60 ms.

For many units, keep the GPU busy with the runner instead of `generate`. `enqueue <unit_dir>` prepares the takes and leaves a request in `.artifacts/h3/queue/`. One `run` process works through the requests a unit at a time: when a unit's samplings finish it frees the models once and queues that unit's decodes with the next unit's samplings right behind them. It then collects, mattes and draws the finished unit while the GPU samples the next one, and prints `READY <unit>` to its log. A retake enqueued with `--join` is added to the unit being sampled. A take that was already submitted is never submitted again, so after a restart, enqueue the unfinished units again. `survey <unit_id> --archive-dir <snapshot>` comes before writing `h3_unit.json`. It restores archived paintings, draws every painted frame with its name, and suggests the scale at which every painting fits the 960x640 video with 40 px to spare. `propose` writes a starting `h3_selection.json` from the motion in the matte frames:
- idle: every second frame.
- move: one gait cycle.
- actions: the moving span with the quiet lead-in and tail trimmed, sampled densely where the figure moves fast, with the contact at the furthest reach toward screen right.

It is a starting point: review it and edit it before `build`. Add the plate sentence "The blue background stays the same flat saturated blue in every frame and never darkens, flashes or changes colour" to every unit, because a plate that drifts toward cyan or black loses its matte.

`prepare` prints each take's unmix guard: the bluest opaque colour in its guides. A unit painted in blue, cyan or violet (ice, crystal) gets a guard far above 100, and the blue plate would eat its figure. Give such a unit a green plate: `"key_rgb": [0, 255, 0]` and "GREEN" in the plate prompt. The matte then runs the same rules with green and blue swapped. For example, the Rimebell Skyraker's guard falls from 150 to 9 and the Sumpstone Guard's from 210 to 50. A runner started before this option mattes every take as blue, so after it finishes a green-plate unit, run `matte` and `sheets` for that unit again, or restart the runner first.

## Validation and retention

Use focused clip/manifest, actual playback, grounding, reduced-motion/Fast and simulation/save checks. No full repository suite. A minimum frame count or a passing structural check is not visual acceptance. Document unfinished identities/clips honestly. Original asset masters, provenance, recipes, saves, backups and caches remain; remove task-owned temporary render previews, logs and probe profiles after inspection. Push coherent validated runtime/content slices to main, preserving unrelated existing work.
